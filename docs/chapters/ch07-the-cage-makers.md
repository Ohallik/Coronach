# Chapter 7 - The Cage Makers (Oriel)

Brief for every stream working on chapter 7. Plan: [SHIPPING_PLAN.md](../SHIPPING_PLAN.md). Story bible: [STORY_CAMPAIGN.md](../STORY_CAMPAIGN.md). How-to: [CHAPTER_GUIDE.md](../CHAPTER_GUIDE.md). Chapters 3-6 come first in the story.

## What the chapter is for

The Muster left Hoarfell with Ancelane Bridle plans. A Bridle is a cage for a young Choir: it holds a living road still so that only its owner can use it. Ishen, Lanternwick's archivist, knows where the plans were first drawn: **Oriel**, the Ancelane homeworld, whose rulers bridled a passage in the last Severance instead of moving with it, and broke their world. If Bridles can be made, they could be unmade, and the record of how is on Oriel if it is anywhere.

Oriel is the strangest and most beautiful place in the game and the quietest chapter: no town, almost no living people, a world still obeying the rule that ruined it. The party comes away with no weapon and no relic, only a **principle a crew can carry out with its hands**: how a Bridle is released without killing what it holds.

- **What the party changes:** the Last Warden, still enforcing the first Bridle around a Choir long since turned to glass, is shown the release and stands down. The first Bridle is opened, a thousand years late.
- **What it gives the convoy:** the release, written down by Ishen and tried by Taren. It is used on the caged juvenile in chapter 8, on Cinder Halo's joints in chapter 9 and on Stillwater's anchor in chapter 10. The Coronach is traced to its source: the leave-taking the people of Oriel never held.
- **What it sets up:** Meret has read the same history and drawn the opposite lesson (hold the road properly this time); she says so over the radio at the end. The Muster's Foundry is already building from the plans.
- **Taren, Sela, Idra:** Sela sees a pinned road from the inside, as a glass corpse the size of a mountain, and her half-agreement with Meret ends without a speech. Taren recognises the Bridle's clamps: they are his trade, a lock is a lock. Idra, who distrusts anything old and wonderful, is the one who notices the gardeners still tending empty gardens and can't shake it.

Sync in: about 19. Sync out: about 22. Play time: 55-70 minutes.

## Places

### Orrery (space, combat) - rule: one readable machine
Oriel's broken moons still ride ancient **light-rails** around the planet like clockwork. Rings of rail-gates open and close in a sequence that can be read from the lights running ahead of each moon. Crossing means timing passes through the gates as moons sweep by: wait in a moon's lee, go when the lights run green. Rail Warden drones patrol the rails and ignore ships that keep the machine's time.

### OrielSkyfields (wild, combat) - rule: weight and light follow the fibres
A near-magical landscape with one visible rule: **where a severed route fibre still runs, weight and light follow it.** Fibres are glowing lines through the air and ground. Islands on a fibre float; rivers run upward along light-threads; glass forests ring. At marked **lift nodes** the fibre carries the party to the next island. Cutting or re-seating a fibre junction (the zone's switch) drops or raises an island, changing the path. The gardeners keep the gardens; the glass forests grew where fibres frayed.

### GlassChoir (dungeon)
A cathedral built around the adult Choir the Ancelane bridled: its body turned to glass around the original Bridle engine, vast, translucent, the nave running along its flank. Four **clamps** hold the dead Choir still. The dungeon is the approach along the body to each clamp's works; the Last Warden's arena is the engine hall under the heart.

**The Last Warden (boss, the engine hall).** A tall Ancelane guardian in bronze and glass with a staff that plucks the fibres. **Its attacks follow the fibre lines**, so reading the lines reads its tells: a fibre brightens, then force runs down it. Phase one: line strikes along single fibres, a fan across three, a pull along a fibre toward its staff. The four **clamps** at the hall's corners are the fight's targets, each opened in an opening (the party performs the release: a short hold interaction while the partner covers). Each clamp released re-routes the hall's fibres and changes the arena: fewer lines, wider floor, and a shaft of light through the glass. With the fourth clamp open the Warden is not beaten down: it **sees the release done**, lowers its staff and asks its question. It stands down.

## Story beats (in order)

1. **Many Hands.** Ishen arrives from Lanternwick with too many books. The plans. Oriel.
2. **The Orrery.** The machine. Ishen, aboard Sela's wake in a courier pod, narrating the moons' names until Idra asks him to stop.
3. **The landing.** Ishen's camp. A gardener watches. The rule, shown before it is said: a stone dropped near a fibre falls sideways.
4. **The Skyfields.** Lift nodes, junctions, gardens. The gardener that follows.
5. **The Glass Choir.** The body. Nobody speaks for a while. Sela: "This is what a held road looks like."
6. **The clamps.** Taren knows them. "It's a lock. A very good one. Somebody was proud of this."
7. **The Last Warden.** The fight. The fourth clamp. "State your office." Taren: "Repairs." It stands down. The first Bridle opens, and the light through the glass moves for the first time in an age.
8. **Ishen reads.** The rulers held the road; the towns that moved held a leave-taking first, and their descendants still do. Lanternwick calls it the Coronach. Oriel never held one.
9. **Meret, by radio.** She knows the history: "They held it badly. I've read the failure reports. I've had longer to prepare than they did." And: the Muster's foundry is running. If the convoy means to prove its way works, it has less time than she thought.
10. **Chapter end.** Brekk, at Lanternwick, has had word from the Unsworn on Scoria: the cage is nearly built, and there is something alive in it. (Chapter 8.)
