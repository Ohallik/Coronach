# Chapter 9 - The House That Can Leave

Brief for every stream working on chapter 9. Plan: [SHIPPING_PLAN.md](../SHIPPING_PLAN.md). Story bible: [STORY_CAMPAIGN.md](../STORY_CAMPAIGN.md), movement IV. How-to: [CHAPTER_GUIDE.md](../CHAPTER_GUIDE.md). Chapters 3-8 come first in the story. This chapter is the heart of the game: it is where the title comes from. **Chapters 9 and 10 are never cut.**

## What the chapter is for

Nacre has fallen in on itself, Stillwater is closing early, and the question the game opened with (did the walkway just move?) is answered: home has to leave. Cinder Halo was assembled from vessels, and the joints Taren has been repairing since the first scene are **separation joints**. The party rescues who it can from Nacre, learns from a town that has never stopped moving how to move one that never has, goes home, and takes the station apart on purpose so that it can travel. Not everything can come. Before the last piece is dismantled, the neighbours hold a **Coronach**: they walk the town and say what the place did for them.

- **What the party changes:** Oda's family and the Nacre navigators are pulled out of the wrecks. The Meridian Crawl's stalled tread section is recovered before the dawn reaches it, and its engineers come to the Halo. The Halo's joints are reopened; the Foundry Saint (the old separation press under a Compact override) is stopped from welding the districts back; the launched districts are defended in the Broken Ring.
- **What it costs:** the continuous promenade cannot survive separation. Hal's workshop cannot be moved whole, and he chooses which machine to take apart himself. Meret's guaranteed berth for the Halo is refused, and she withdraws it.
- **What it sets up:** the Halo's districts are now ships in the convoy. Stillwater's clamp is tightening around Sorrel, on the only shared exit: the finale starts where the game did.
- **Taren, Sela, Idra:** Taren dismantles his own good work so that others can leave, and shares the job instead of being the one who always fixes things. Sela, who has been saving for a one-way departure since before the game began, teaches the Nacre navigators and the lantern-keepers her charts so that the convoy doesn't depend on one surveyor: she makes herself replaceable and finds she minds less than she expected. Idra holds the Ring.

Sync in: about 25. Sync out: about 27. Play time: 80-100 minutes.

## Places

### NacreGrave (space, combat) - rule: a wreck field with a spiral
Fresh wrecks from the berth disaster turning through an older salvage spiral: the single huge transfer berth broken open at the centre, ferries and haulers in slow orbit around it, family hull colours, the Choir's passage a bruise of light beyond. The spiral is the lane: inward is faster and more dangerous (debris on marked tracks, a Bridle fragment still thrashing on a tether), outward is slow and safe. Find three beacons: **Oda's hauler**, the **tug *Welcome Back***  (Bryn Asta's crew), and the navigators' pod. Tow-drones gone wrong are still dragging hulls toward the destroyed berth.

### MeridianCrawl (town, safe) and Terminator (wild, a run against the dawn)
**Duskline** is a tidally locked world with a habitable twilight band. The **Meridian Crawl** is a town on enormous treads that keeps ahead of the dawn and has never stopped: tread-halls, a swaying market street, a sky-garden deck, the helm. The horizon light always shifts. One tread section has stalled and been left behind on the day side. **The Terminator** is the run to recover it: an 800 m band of twilight surface with the **lethal dawn line advancing visibly** behind the party, heat-scavengers attacking, and three couplings to re-mate before the section can be driven back. There is no boss. It is an escape set piece with checkpoints at each coupling, and failure restarts from the last coupling, never the start.

### The Decks on separation day (the existing town, changed)
The same streets with deliberate state changes: evacuation crates stacked on the promenade, shutters down on shops whose owners have already gone aboard, chalk lines along the joints, Crawl engineers in the service hatches, families at the airlocks, the fruit stall where it always was. New access opens **beneath the promenade**.

### SeparationWorks (dungeon)
The station's original separation plant, under the promenade, exposing what Cinder Halo is made of: tug bows, hauler ribs, six different hull standards joined by the collars Taren has been repairing. Six joints to reopen (the chapter-wide switch; three are on the critical path), press galleries, a control room. The **Foundry Saint** stands in the main press hall.

### BrokenRing (space, combat) - rule: escorting departing town sections
The station coming apart around the party: districts lifting away one by one on Crawl-rigged engines, each a named place (the market, the repair quarter, the residential crescent, the tug dock), lit windows, family colours. Debris moves through marked channels. The party flies beside the sections; the **Debris Cutter** hunts them. No escort health bar and no civilian-loss score: the fight is against the Cutter, and the sections are landmarks and cover.

**Foundry Saint (boss, the press hall).** Cinder Halo's old separation press, held upright by six heavy arms, older than anyone living; a Compact override makes it try to reattach the districts. Phase one: two arms slam marked rectangles, a third drags a welding line across the hall, and the press head drops on a marked circle. **Each arm is a break target at its shoulder collar**, and the collars are Taren's trade: breaking one doesn't destroy it, it *releases* it (Oriel's principle, on home's own machinery). With three arms released the override rig is exposed on the Saint's back: phase two is a climb along the fallen arms to the rig while the last three arms sweep. Destroy the rig and the Saint settles and returns to local control: Orrin's voice on its speaker, running the separation it was built for.

**Debris Cutter (boss, the Broken Ring).** A Compact interception craft weaving through the departing sections with three weapon pods: a cutting beam (a marked lane), a mine-layer (marked spheres in the channels), and a tractor (pulls the party off line). **The player chooses which pod to disable first**, and its pursuit changes accordingly. A sustained flight duel among named landmarks. Disabled, it is towed off by Bryn Asta's tug; its crew live.

## Story beats (in order)

1. **Nacre.** The distress calls. The spiral. Oda's hauler: "You said to find you." "I said to find me. You're the one who got lost." Bryn Asta. The navigators.
2. **Many Hands.** Meret's all-stations notice: Stillwater proceeds; the Halo is offered a guaranteed berth in the shelter corridor if it cuts the outlying convoy loose. Idra: a town can't be moved by people who've never moved one. Maru knows who has.
3. **Duskline.** The Crawl. Dorrow. The stalled section and the dawn. The run.
4. **Home.** The Decks, crated. Orrin is tempted, and says so.
5. **The town meeting.** Short. Meret attends and makes the offer herself. Hal asks how many apprentices the berth includes. Mira demands the manifest. Ilo reads the footnotes: the people in the room whose names aren't on it. Oda stands up. Orrin tells the room what he postponed. The Halo refuses the berth. Meret: "Then I withdraw it. I'm sorry. I mean that."
6. **The Works.** The joints. What the station is made of. The Saint.
7. **Hal.** The machine he takes apart himself. Taren helps, and for once only holds the lamp.
8. **The Coronach.** An unhurried walk through the town before the last joint opens. No combat, no timer. Each neighbour says what the place did for them (Mira: the stall; Orrin: the board; Neve: a lane to come home down; Oda: line nine hundred and four, framed; Jun: the pears; Pell, Ruva, Maru, Tern's herders, Tovra, Dorrow as guests who know what it costs). Side stories completed earlier add lines and people. Taren and Sela say theirs last. Music: a new piece (see below).
9. **The launch.** The districts lift. The promenade parts. Neve flies the first one out.
10. **The Broken Ring.** The Debris Cutter. Idra holds the line.
11. **Chapter end.** The Halo is a fleet. Sela's chart: every road out now runs through one passage, and Stillwater's clamp is closing on it at Sorrel, around the nursery. Back to the first moon. (Chapter 10.)
