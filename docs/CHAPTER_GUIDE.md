# Coronach - chapter guide

How a chapter is put together in this codebase, derived from how chapter 2 (The Lantern Road, D202: `WickReach`, `Lanternwick`, `LanternGorge`, `SunkenRelay`, boss `HessaKorl`) was built. Plan: [SHIPPING_PLAN.md](SHIPPING_PLAN.md). Each chapter's content brief: `docs/chapters/chNN-*.md`. Commands and session safety: [NEXT_SESSION_PROMPT.md](NEXT_SESSION_PROMPT.md). Checked against the scripts and tests committed at `ff99a7a` (November 11; unchanged at `70264d7`). Line numbers drift; names don't. What could not be confirmed is listed in [section 14](#14-unverified).

**Updated the same day for the framework pass** (uncommitted when written): sections 1, 3.2, 3.8, 3.9, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11 and 13 describe zones, music, objective lines, enemies, bosses, routes and tests as registered from data and from each region's own files. Those parts were type-checked (`python tools/typecheck.py`, both dags) and `tools/route_lint.py` was run on all 88 routes; nothing in them has been run in Unity yet.

**In flight while this was written:** the systems streams (S1 impact, S2 loot, S3 skills) had uncommitted edits across `S/Combat`, `S/Rpg` and `S/Data`. Where this guide and the code disagree about shops, loot, items, consumables or hit weight, the code is newer; the places known to be moving are marked "S2".

Short paths: `S/` = `Lattice/Assets/_Project/Scripts/`, `A/` = `Lattice/Assets/_Project/`, `T/` = `Lattice/Assets/Tests/`. The worked example throughout is `S/Editor/World/LanternRoadBuilder.cs` (LRB).

## 0. Ground rules

- **Several engineers share this working tree and its Unity** (since November 12; before that only the lead started Unity). Three rules make that work:
  - **Keep the tree compiling.** Run `python tools/typecheck.py` after each batch of edits and before any Unity run. Add the new thing, move callers and tests to it, then remove the old; never leave the tree red for longer than a minute. Edit your own files; a shared file (a registration list, `ContentRegistry.cs`, this guide) gets a small append with an exact edit, never a rewrite.
  - **Builders, renders and intake run in the main project**, one at a time, through `& .\scripts\exec-when-green.ps1 -Method Lattice.EditorTools.<Method> -Marker <MARKER> [-Graphics] [-TimeoutSec n] [-ExtraArgs @(...)]`. It waits for a tree that compiles and for its turn at Unity. Afterwards: `git checkout -- Lattice/Packages/packages-lock.json Lattice/Assets/_Project/Resources/WorldMaterials/staged-light-line.mat`.
  - **Tests run in a test mirror**, a second copy of the project with its own Unity and its own save folder (`..\CoronachTest\A`, `C`; made by `scripts/mirror-sync.ps1`): `.\scripts\focused.ps1 -SkipVerify -Label <yours> -Classes 'ATests|BTests'`. It copies the tree across when it compiles (waiting out a running builder), runs, and puts the result in `Builds/quality/focused/<label>-playmode.xml`; anything else a test writes stays under the mirror's `Builds`. The whole suite is `.\scripts\suite-shared.ps1 -Label <name>` (mirror `S`, one frozen copy, about 2.5 hours). Start either in the background and wait for it to finish; don't poll in a sleep loop.
  - **Only the lead** commits, builds players and runs anything with a player window (`quality-*.ps1`, `look.ps1`, replays): those need the machine to themselves. Nobody runs `headless.ps1 -Mode playtests` or `full-suite.ps1` in the main project, stops a Unity they didn't start, or edits under `CoronachTest`.
  - **Proving a test can fail never breaks a shared file.** Other engineers' test runs copy the tree at any moment (three deliberate breaks reached another run that way on November 12). Put the broken state inside the test (build the fixture wrong first and require the failure, then right), or behind a switch that is off unless your own run sets it, and remove the switch afterwards.
  - **While you wait for a queued run, stop with one line.** Report once, when the work is built, seen and tested.
  - **The scratchpad folder is shared too.** Every engineer's temporary files land in the same folder, and a plain name like `coords.md` or `sheet.py` has been overwritten by someone else's more than once. Make a subfolder of your own on your first write and keep everything in it.
- **Scenes, prefabs, meshes, navigation and most definitions are build output.** Nobody edits a `.unity`, `.prefab` or generated `.asset` by hand: change the builder and have it rebuilt. A rebuild starts each scene from an empty one, so anything not in code is lost.
- **Isolated entry points only.** Never run `WorldBuilder.BuildAll/BuildFinal`, `ArenaBuilder.Build`, `WorldBuilder.Prepare` or `GenIntake.Build` for chapter work: they regenerate chapter 1's definitions and scenes and strip their staging.
- **Evidence rules:** prove a new check fails on the broken state first, never loosen an assertion, never claim unobserved quality. Record consequential decisions in `docs/DECISIONS.md` (next free on November 11: D232; NEXT_SESSION_PROMPT keeps the number).
- **A screen never prints a raw id** (D200). Every id a player could read goes through `S/Core/DisplayNames.cs`.

### 0.1 The bar, and the faults already made once

The lead looks at every zone's stills and every fight's numbers before accepting them. Check your own work against this list first.

**A place** (stills from `LookDev.Render`, looked at as contact sheets with `tools/look_contact.py` or `tools/contact_sheet.py`, single stills opened where a sheet shows a problem):
- No plain floor: `python tools/look_density.py`, every play-zoom ground still at or under .22, each room in the state a player stands in it.
- No texture stretched into stripes, no flat-shaded lump standing in for rock or growth, no saw-tooth edge between floor and wall, nothing magenta or flat purple (it reads as a missing shader).
- A space backdrop is crisp at the flight camera's scale (no soft planet, no pixel steps), and every flight frame has a subject. Debris has several silhouettes.
- Each room, district or stretch has something of its own to look at; a town has a landmark you can name from one still.
- People: the bodies of the people who live there, at least three looks per group, no two neighbours alike, and most of them with somewhere to be (`WalkLoop`, `Whereabouts`).
- What you build in code holds up beside the generated pieces next to it.
- A flight zone's rule is readable from the screen, not from text.
- A danger marking is never hidden by a decal, by water or by anything floating on it.
- A place is lit by what it is about. Passing the density check is not the look: four zones in a row passed it and came back (the foundry had no heat in it, the lava field's melt was paler than its crust, the glass cathedral was a sunlit sand courtyard). The test for a still, with no caption: would a stranger say what kind of place this is and what it is beside? Indoors, the ambient comes down and the subject (the furnace, the glass, the lamps) is the brightest thing in frame; heroes, prompts and danger markings must still win in the darkest room.
- The ground kit's path layer (irregular pavers on sand) is for outdoor wild ground. A built floor is laid in courses by whoever built the place, and says who they were; grit drifts into corners, it doesn't blend across half a room.
- A room of the same thing for a hundred metres needs a beat (columns, shadows, lamps) and one place worth stopping at.

**What the shared tests reject in every zone.** Before reporting, run your zone's cases of them: `.\scripts\focused.ps1 -SkipVerify -Label <yours> -Classes '<YourOwnTests>' -Zones 'YourZone,YourOtherZone'` runs your own class plus every shared case that names those zones, in a few minutes. Don't run the shared classes whole (`QaPlanWorldTests`, `QaWorldSecondTests`, `QaChapterOneLookTests`, `QaChapterTwoLookTests`, `QaStoryWorldTests`, `QaPlanEarlyTests`, `QaPlanSystemsTests`, `QaUiFlowTests`, `QaWorldBeatsTests`, `QaLookAndSoundTests`): they walk every zone in the game, take over an hour and hold a test mirror all that time; the lead's suite runs them. Since November 12 every check in them that walks the game's zones is one case per zone, so `-Zones` finds all of it, and a zone that can't load or logs an error of its own (a scene not in the build yet, a track not staged) fails its own cases and nobody else's. What compares zones with each other is a test of its own named `...AcrossZones` (`T/PlayMode/ZoneWalk.cs`: it takes what each zone's case read earlier in the same run, reads the rest itself, and leaves a zone it can't load out by name). Those, and the few that never took a zone, are the shared tests that take no zone. The ones new content can break are covered by adding `|Lattice.Tests.PlayMode.QaPlanWorldTests.EveryExitNamesWhereItGoesAndItsArrivalIsThereAcrossZones|Lattice.Tests.PlayMode.QaPlanWorldTests.EveryLockedExitStaysShutAndSaysWhatOpensItAcrossZones|Lattice.Tests.PlayMode.QaPlanWorldTests.EveryConversationLockAndQuestStepIsWiredToSomethingInTheWorldAcrossZones|Lattice.Tests.PlayMode.QaPlanWorldTests.NoQuestMovesOnOutOfTurn|Lattice.Tests.PlayMode.QaPlanWorldTests.EveryQuestsCardsFollowItsStepsAndItsRewardIsPaidOnce|Lattice.Tests.PlayMode.QaPlanSystemsTests.ItemNamesAreSpelledOneWayEverywhere|Lattice.Tests.PlayMode.QaChapterOneLookTests.FloorSignsReadFromTheFixedCameraOnAnyFloorAcrossZones|Lattice.Tests.PlayMode.QaLookAndSoundTests.EachNaturalPlaceHasItsOwnAirAcrossZones|DialogueInputTests` to `-Classes` once, near the end (exits landing at arrivals in other zones, the Reach's lock and the count of locks, quest steps and locks wired across the world, quest order and cards, item spelling, one material for every sign, no two places with the same air, dialogue; each `...AcrossZones` run alone loads every zone, a minute or two). Name the `...AcrossZones` test, not the old name: a filter matches anywhere in a test's name, so `...QaPlanWorldTests.EveryLockedExitStaysShutAndSaysWhatOpensIt` now also runs every zone's case of it. The other seven (`NoPlaceholderIsDrawnInAnyZone`, `NoKitPieceIsStretchedAcrossItsTexture`, `NoRawIdOrWrongWordOnAnyPromptOrSign`, `EverySignReadsLevelToTheCamera`, `WorldLabelsReadFromTheGamesCameraAtTheTypeFloor`, `NoNameIsPrintedOnAFloorSign`, `WarpSignsReadFromTheGamesCameraAtTheTypeFloor`, each with `AcrossZones` after it) only count what the measures read over the whole world, which new content can't lower; the lead's suite runs them. What they reject:
- An edge of the world with nothing visible to stop you, or an invisible wall across ground that visibly carries on.
- Anything wide and tall between the fixed camera and a place where a fight or a talk happens; a speaker hidden behind scenery.
- A townsperson or a prop on a way people must walk (check everything the builder places against its declared ways, not only its scatter).
- A prompt or sign that prints a raw id or the wrong form of a name (it is always "Keeper Abri"); an item spelled differently in a script than on screen.
- A quest step that can be done out of turn (two steps that report the same action); a locked way that doesn't say what opens it; a way out with no sign.
- A quest whose card names something the bag doesn't receive. A capped consumable given to a full pouch is paid as scrip instead, so a quest's reward is materials, parts and scrip, never a capped consumable.
- A new chapter's beats in `SideStories.Beats` winning in an earlier chapter (chapter 3's block once replaced chapter 2's last talk).
- A mesh with NaN bounds: clamp before any power of a sine or a square root of a difference.

**A fight:**
- Every ordinary enemy has one clear tell and one clear answer, different from its neighbours'. An existing behaviour family tuned by data comes before new code.
- Damage is authored at its real weight with the zone's `enemyThreat` at 1. An ordinary hit takes 9 to 13 percent of a hero's bar at the Sync and in the parts a player has there, measured through `Health.Receive`; a boss's moves take 12 to 16. Show the arithmetic.
- A chapter's boss lasts two to five minutes for the real party (`DevLoadout chapter<N>`, which the shared length test uses from chapter 3 on); aim for about three. A mini-boss states its own bounds on its sheet.
- A boss teaches its moves, changes the room or itself at its shift, and has openings the player earns. Every area attack is marked; nothing important stands behind the boss from the fixed camera; its weak spot can be reached by the weapon meant to reach it.
- Break targets cannot be hit or targeted before the fight starts. A boss that survives leaves through a placed `BossExit`. A boss doesn't bark over a scripted talk.
- A capsule is at least twice its radius tall (Unity stretches a shorter one and the body floats). A Guardian's arc is under 180. Every sound but the tell goes through `EnemyBehaviour.Sound`.
- A track a definition names must be staged (`MusicIntake.Stage`) before any test wakes the enemy that plays it.

**Working together:** before you write to a file you didn't create, check it isn't another engineer's new work (a file was lost that way). A contract between two engineers lives in the zone's map doc under its own heading; the one who needs something writes it, the one who builds it writes the coordinates back.

## 2. House style

- **Assemblies** (one `.asmdef` per folder under `S/`): `Lattice.Data` <- `Core` <- `Rpg` <- `Combat` <- `World`; `Dialogue` sits beside Combat (Data, Core, Rpg); `UI` sees all of them and `Editor` sees everything. World sees Combat; Combat never sees World, Dialogue or UI, which is why `ObjectiveHud` and `PeopleMarks` live in UI. Namespaces match (`Lattice.World`), except editor code, which is `Lattice.EditorTools`. Tests are `Lattice.Tests.PlayMode` / `.EditMode`.
- **Dense code.** No spaces around operators or after commas, several statements on a line, expression-bodied members, `var`, target-typed `new()`, tuples and `switch` expressions, local functions for one zone's helpers (`void Fight(...)`). Four-space indent, Allman braces for multi-line blocks, one-line `{...}` bodies. Lines of 150 to 300 characters are normal. A few files are conventionally spaced instead (`MusicDirector.cs`, `ContinuousReview.cs`, parts of `StationRedesign.cs`): match the file you are in.
- **Comments say why, in what a player saw.** Most record the fault that led to the line ("a post stood across Corra's legs for her whole conversation"). `/// <summary>` on types and on anything non-obvious. No commented-out code.
- **Naming.** Public serialized fields are lower camel (`requiredFlag`); constants and `static readonly` are Pascal (`TallOverAFight`); private statics lower camel. Prompts read "Verb — Place" with an em dash (`"Launch — The Wick Reach"`). Floor signs are upper case (`"ARCH MARKET"`).
- **One scene MonoBehaviour per file**, the file named after the class. Unity resolves a serialized component by file name.
- **Serialized enums are append-only** (`SkillKind`, `PortraitEmotion`, and in practice `ObjectiveKind`, `EnemyArchetype`, `ZoneKind`: assets store the ordinal).
- **Editor entry points** are `public static void X()=>BatchTools.Run(()=>{...;Debug.Log("X_OK");});`. The marker is the method's last log line and `exec.ps1 -Marker` waits for it. Arguments come through `DevArgs.Value("-flag")` / `DevArgs.Has("-flag")`.
- **Gameplay logs** are upper-case markers tests listen for: `ZONE_ENTER`, `DISCOVERY`, `FLAG_ALL`, `RELAY_LIT`, `ENCOUNTER_CLEAR`, `BOSS_MOVE`, `BOSS_SHIFT`, `BOSS_DOWN`, `BOSS_DURATION`.
- **Randomness in builders is seeded** (`new System.Random(41)`), and a scatter *drops* what it must keep off rather than moving it, so later draws stay in step and every replay route still walks the same ground (D213). Replacing a kit key in a scatter list goes in the old key's slot for the same reason.
- **`.meta` files are committed**, one per file and folder. Unity writes them. A new `.cs` may be committed with a two-line meta (`fileFormatVersion: 2` / `guid: <32 hex, new>`) or left for the lead's next Unity run to create; never reuse or edit an existing GUID. See section 14.
- **Python tools:** stdlib plus Pillow, a usage docstring at the top, a final `*_OK` line.
- **Commits:** message from a file (`git commit -F`). Revert `Lattice/Packages/packages-lock.json` and `A/Resources/WorldMaterials/staged-light-line.mat` first. Never commit `Builds/`, `Library/`, `art-src/`, `tools/meshy/.env`.

## 3. Zones

### 3.1 The builder file

One static class per region in `S/Editor/World/`, with these entry points (LRB lines 33-72):

```csharp
public static class XylosBuilder
{
    public static readonly string[] Zones={"XylosNearspace","Rootmarket","BraidedRiver","WalkingCanopy"};
    public static void Definitions()=>BatchTools.Run(()=>{Define();AssetDatabase.SaveAssets();Debug.Log("XYLOS_DEFS_OK");});
    public static void BuildZones()=>BatchTools.Run(()=>
    {
        string arg=DevArgs.Value("-xylos-zones");var zones=string.IsNullOrEmpty(arg)?Zones:arg.Split(',',StringSplitOptions.RemoveEmptyEntries);
        Define();foreach(var zone in zones)BuildZone(zone.Trim());
        BatchTools.RegisterScenes();AssetDatabase.SaveAssets();Debug.Log("XYLOS_ZONES_OK "+string.Join(",",zones));
    });
    public static void Dialogue()=>BatchTools.Run(()=>{RefreshDialogue();Debug.Log("XYLOS_DIALOGUE_OK");});
}
```

`Define()` always runs before a zone is built, because `WorldBuilder.Begin` reads the `ZoneDef`. A way into the region from an existing scene is staged by opening that scene and replacing only its own objects (LRB `StageTallowApproach`, entry `StageApproach`): destroy the old warp and spawn by query, add the new ones, save. Register the entry point with `[Restage.Step]` (section 12.1), or the next rebuild of that scene loses it.

**LRB's helpers are private.** `Terrain`, `Prop`, `Kit`, `Scatter`, `CliffFoot`, `SeatInWall`, `HidesFloor`, `Gate`, `Person`, `Discovery`, `Relay`, `Lantern`, `Lamp`, `Arch`, `CurtainMaterial`, `Strand`, `BakeNavigation`, `OnASign`, `InSightOf` and the state they share (`height`, `CliffTop`, `fadeTall`) cannot be called from another class today, and `Terrain` hard-wires the canyon cliff material. Until the lead lifts them into a shared class, a region builder carries its own copies. Don't fork them four ways without saying so.

### 3.2 The ZoneDef

`S/Data/ZoneDef.cs`. The asset's file name, `id` and `scene` are the same string (`GameCatalog.Find<T>(id)` loads `Resources/Definitions/<Type>/<id>`).

```csharp
var ground=Find<CameraProfile>("Ground");var flight=Find<CameraProfile>("Flight");
var dusk=ArenaBuilder.Asset<Hd2dProfile>("Dusk",p=>{p.tint=new Color(1,.9f,.82f);p.tiltStart=.2f;p.tiltStrength=0;p.nativeResolution=true;p.bloom=.5f;});
ArenaBuilder.Asset<ZoneDef>("LanternGorge",z=>{z.id=z.scene="LanternGorge";z.kind=ZoneKind.GroundCombat;z.cameraProfile=ground;z.hd2dProfile=dusk;z.skybox=sky;z.enemyThreat=1.6f;z.cameraClearsScenery=true;});
```

`ArenaBuilder.Asset<T>(string id,Action<T> configure)` loads or creates the asset and runs the lambda. Fields the lambda doesn't name survive a rebuild (which is how `ambientAudio` persists); fields it names are overwritten every run, so **tune in the builder, never in the asset**.

| Field | Use |
|---|---|
| `kind` | `GroundSafe` (Natural bodies, no combat), `GroundCombat` (Shaped), `SpaceSafe` (CivilFlight), `SpaceCombat` (Flight). `ZoneController` derives form, input mode and combat from it |
| `cameraProfile` | `Ground` (pitch 40, yaw 20, distance 19.5) or `Flight` (pitch 48, yaw 0, distance 26). Both exist; don't make more |
| `hd2dProfile` | must not be null (`WorldBuilder.Begin` reads `.bloom`). Existing: `Warm`, `Cold`, `DeckSoft`, `Halo`, `Tallow`, `Cave`, `Dusk` |
| `skybox` | a material, or null for underground (camera clears to solid colour) |
| `enemyThreat` | multiplies ordinary enemies' damage here; bosses stay at 1 (D194). Chapter 2: 1.5, 1.6, 1.7 |
| `cameraClearsScenery` | true on open landforms: the camera slides in rather than hide the party. Stations and flight zones leave it off |
| `flightPlane` | 1 in every flight zone |
| `ambientAudio`, `ambientVolume` | the zone's bed; no builder sets it (section 14) |
| `musicId` | the zone's track, a title from `docs/music/tracks.json`. Required on every zone since chapter 2 (section 3.8) |
| `fightMusicId`, `pocketMusicId`, `shopMusicId` | optional: what plays while an ordinary encounter is awake near the party, inside a `SafePocket` of a combat zone, and with a shop open (empty: "Moonbase Market") |
| `displayName` | what a player reads ("The Wick Reach"). Empty: the id split into words |
| `brightFloor` | sunlit pale ground: floor signs take the lighter plate |
| `chapter` | 3 to 10. New scenes join the build list in this order, and the test registry orders zones by it |

### 3.5 Terrain and the fixed camera

`Terrain` turns one height function into three saved meshes: an invisible full-resolution collider (`<Zone>-Terrain.asset`, also the single welded shadow caster), the walkable floor (`-Ground`, flatter than normal.y .78 and below `CliffTop`) and the walls (`-Cliffs`, strata mapped along each wall). The floor's vertex colour is the tone function (a paler worn path, a market square), darkened toward wall feet. Use .5 m cells: at 1 m every wall foot is a row of teeth. Rules the chapter 2 comments paid for:

- The ground camera sits south-west of the hero and never turns (D119, D212). **West and south walls are its foreground: keep them low** (`walls*=.42f` for x<0 in town; `cameraSide?.36f:1` in the relay) or the camera closes in on the hero's knees.
- It sees about 7 to 9 m north of the hero. A wall meant to stand behind a speaker has to come that close; a talk trigger goes just inside the door.
- An arrival opens on the room, not on a wall behind the party (camera distance over 14 at every spawn).
- Nothing 7 m or more across and more than 3 m over the floor stands on the camera's line to a fight or the walked path (`HidesFloor`, `TallOverAFight=3`). Kit rocks are capped at 8 m wide and seated into the wall (`SeatInWall`).
- In a ground zone any prop 2.5 m or taller gets `SceneryOcclusion` and its material's `_OCCFADE_ON` (`fadeTall`). An arch you walk through declares its `opening`.
- Nothing stands on a floor sign's ground or between the camera and a named person (`OnASign`, `InSightOf`). Each zone lists its own signs and people for its scatter.
- In flight the camera looks down at 48 degrees, so a thing below the lane shows south of where it is: clear backdrop by where it shows (LRB `ClearOnScreen`).
- `WorldBuilder.Label` signs are restyled at load by `S/UI/SignStyle.cs`. Put a floor sign at y about .1 beside what it names. A zone with sunlit pale floors sets `ZoneDef.brightFloor`.
- Every word in the world, a sign or a person's name, is drawn `SignStyle.Reach` (6 m) nearer the camera along its own lines of sight (`Lattice/SignText`, `Lattice/SignPlate`): a lamp, a well, a crate or an awning standing in front of it never cuts it. A wall taller than about 3.8 m in front of it still does, and so does a doorway's lintel more than 6 m short of it along the camera's line (a floor word lying 6 m past an arch is half under the lintel when the party stands back across the room), so lay a sign where no building stands between it and the camera from anywhere it is on the screen, in front of an arch rather than past it. What is behind it is read from the play camera as the zone loads: on ground pale all through it is printed in dark ink with a pale halo, otherwise white on the plate that ground needs (a plate never draws over a danger marking, D349); a name has a dark tag in its own text and faces the camera whatever its body is doing; a place's name steps aside in a fight and under a body standing on it. Don't print a floating label over a painted sign that already says the same, and lay a label on or against what it names, not in the open beside it. `WorldLabelTests` reads every zone's words from the play camera, each from the party three metres short of it and again from where the party stands for every other word whose view holds it: one cut, turned or under 3 to 1 against what is behind it, from any of those, fails that zone's case, and the view it was cut from is kept in `Builds/quality/labels/why`.
- Underground, light comes from fixtures you can see: every `Lamp` has a prop under it, hung out over the room rather than against a wall (`OffTheWall`). One floor renderer takes about six lights; past that, paint a pool (`ChapterOneStaging.Soft`, `SoftPatch`) instead of adding a light.

### 3.6 Spawns, exits and locks

- Every zone needs `WorldBuilder.Spawn("Arrival",...)`. Each way in names a spawn id that exists in the target scene. A spawn needs floor, room for two bodies (the companion stands 2 m to +X) and, on foot, navigation within 1.5 m.
- `Gate(label,at,scene,spawn,flag,locked,key,size,range,signAt)`: `flag` null means open; `locked` is what the prompt says when refused and must name what opens it. A tall gate's prompt measures to the middle of its body, so its `range` grows with its size.
- `WorldBuilder.Warp`'s default `flag` is `"warpkey"`: pass `""` and clear `lockedPrompt` for an open warp. `WarpBeacon`, `DockingPad`, `RepairBay` and `Bench` refuse while a boss fight is under way or an enemy is awake nearby.
- Entering a zone autosaves (`SceneFlow`), except `Title` and `Arena_*`.

### 3.7 Navigation

`BakeNavigation` reads only **static, non-trigger `BoxCollider` and `MeshCollider`** (not children of a `Membrane`), marks each `Npc`'s collider not walkable, bakes with radius .55, height 2, climb .3, slope 35, voxel .15, and **throws** if the arrival has no floor or any `mustReach` point has no complete path (log `LANTERN_ROUTE_BAKE_OK <zone> points=N` on success). It saves `<Zone>-Navigation.asset` and adds a `GroundNavigation` object. List every place a player must stand: each named person, each pick-up, each door, the boss floor. A capsule or sphere collider is invisible to the bake, which is why a townsperson stands in a static box. A seal that closes mid-zone carries a carving `NavMeshObstacle`, disabled while open (`Membrane.SetOpen` toggles it).

### 3.8 Light, music, ambience

- **Light.** Register each zone's look from the region's own builder file, in a static method marked `[ZoneLook.Source]`; nobody edits `ZoneLook.Specs` (it holds the first thirteen):

```csharp
[ZoneLook.Source] static void Looks()
{
    ZoneLook.Register("Rootmarket",new ZoneLook.Spec{sun=new Color(1,.93f,.8f),sunIntensity=1.3f,sunPitch=40,sky=new Color(.44f,.52f,.6f),equator=new Color(.4f,.42f,.36f),ground=new Color(.2f,.22f,.15f),
        fog=true,fogColor=new Color(.6f,.7f,.6f),contrast=10,saturation=4,bloomIntensity=.5f,vignette=.22f});
}
```

  A `Spec` is sun colour, intensity and pitch, trilight ambient, fog, exposure, contrast, saturation, split toning, bloom and vignette. The method is found by its attribute whichever entry point runs. **`WorldBuilder.Save` throws, naming the zone, for a zone with no spec**, and `ZoneLook.Apply -look-zones X` throws for a name it doesn't know, so a zone can no longer ship in the builder's flat light because someone forgot. `ZoneLook.Apply` needs exactly one directional light and one global volume in the scene, and must still be rerun after every rebuild because `Begin` resets the light to flat (a test now catches a later zone left flat). Lessons: a warm sun wants a cooler fill and grade or stone goes one flat orange (Lanternwick); a cave is a faint cold fill too weak to cast a shadow, lit by its lamps (SunkenRelay, Hushwell). No blur, ever (D060).
- **Music.** Name it on the definition: `z.musicId="Seed Shakers of Xylos"` (a `title` from `docs/music/tracks.json`), and optionally `z.fightMusicId` (plays while an ordinary encounter in this zone is awake within 60 m of the party and gives the zone's music back when it ends or is left behind: the plan's travel music, then "Starfight"), `z.pocketMusicId` (inside a `SafePocket` of a combat zone) and `z.shopMusicId` (with a shop open; empty is "Moonbase Market"). A boss plays "Alien Boss Battle" unless its `EnemyDef.bossMusicId` names another, and takes precedence over a fight cue. That is the whole registration: `MusicIntake.Stage` stages every track any `ZoneDef` or boss `EnemyDef` names, and throws `MUSIC_UNSOURCED`, listing who names what, if a title isn't in `tracks.json` or its source file (`Music/<Title>.ogg`) is missing; it still checks the sha256. A definition naming a track is the assignment: the row's `"status"` needn't be changed. `MusicDirector.SetLocation`'s own branches and `MusicIntake.SliceTracks` are the first thirteen zones' and take no additions. A later zone with no `musicId` logs `MUSIC_UNSET`, and fails `ContentFrameworkTests` and `EachZonePlaysThePlansTrack`.
- **Ambience.** `ZoneDef.ambientAudio` plays as the zone's bed (clips in `A/Audio/Ambience/`); with none, a ground zone hums `computerNoise_000`. Natural places need a bed of their own.

### 3.9 Registration checklist for a new zone

Everything a new zone needs is in the region's own builder file. Nothing in `BatchTools`, `ZoneLook.cs`, `MusicDirector`, `MusicIntake`, `DisplayNames`, `ObjectiveHud` or `SignStyle` is edited.

1. `ArenaBuilder.Asset<ZoneDef>("<Zone>",...)` with `id`, `scene` and the file name identical (the scene registration throws if they differ), and on it: `chapter`, `musicId` (and `fightMusicId`, `pocketMusicId`, `shopMusicId` where wanted), `displayName` when the id split into words isn't the name ("The Wick Reach"), `brightFloor` for sunlit pale ground.
2. A `ZoneLook.Spec` registered from the builder (`[ZoneLook.Source]`, section 3.8). `WorldBuilder.Save` refuses the zone without it.
3. The build list needs nothing: `BatchTools.RegisterScenes()` (already called by `BuildZones` and by every player build) keeps the first fifteen scenes at their indices and appends every other `ZoneDef` whose scene file exists, by `chapter` and then id. A zone defined and not yet built logs `SCENE_UNBUILT`.
4. The objective line needs nothing: with no hand-written case a zone shows the open main quest's current step (section 8), or the last one's `closingLine`, or the zone's name. It never shows the yard's "Practice attacks, dodges and swaps".
5. An ambience bed on the `ZoneDef` (still set by hand on the asset: section 14).
6. `docs/maps/<Zone>.md`. The tests find the zone by themselves (section 11.2).
7. In-player stills need nothing: `scripts/look.ps1` and `S/UI/ZoneLookProbe.cs` take any built zone. [lead] `.\scripts\look.ps1 -Zones Rootmarket,BraidedRiver -Loadout chapter3 -Folder Builds/logs/look/<name>` writes `<Zone>-0.png` to `<Zone>-2.png` for each (the dev player must have been built after the zone; a zone it doesn't hold fails the run by name). With no `-Zones` it takes every built zone: chapter 1's six first, with the viewpoints set for them by hand in the probe, then each other `ZoneDef` whose scene exists, by `chapter` and then id. A later zone is taken at its `Arrival` spawn and at the two places furthest from it among its other spawns, its prompts and its fights (walkable points on foot, the flight plane in flight). `python tools/look_contact.py [<folder>] [--zones A,B]` makes the review sheets.
8. A settlement (somewhere with a shop, a bench or people to come back to) gets a `ChartStop`, so the party can travel back to it by Sela's chart: section 3.10. A zone that isn't one needs nothing, beyond being listed in its settlement's `reaches`.

Then, lead: `Lattice.EditorTools.ContentScaffold.Check` (marker `CONTENT_OK`; `-content-unbuilt` passes over zones not built yet) lists everything a definition names that isn't there: a zone with no look, music, chapter or scene, a scene not in the build list, an unstaged track, an unregistered boss, a quest whose step names a zone, speaker or enemy that doesn't exist, a chart stop that arrives in no zone or at no spawn, stands in another's name or can't be reached with the d-pad.

### 3.10 Sela's chart: a stop for each settlement

The pause menu's **Chart** page is fast travel between settlements (`S/UI/RouteChart.cs`, drawn into `PauseMenu`; field art from `tools/route_chart_art.py`). It exists once `xylos.charted` is set, chapter 3's first scene: until then the menu is the seven tabs it always was. Its tab stands after Quests but its number is `PauseMenu.ChartPage=7`, after Settings' 6, so no older page changed number: **a page's number is not its place in the tab column** (`PauseMenu.Order` is). Each settlement is one `ChartStop` (`S/Data/ChartStop.cs`), defined in the region's `Define()`; nothing else is edited. What `XylosBuilder.Define()` owes for Rootmarket, exactly (the blurb is the chapter's to reword):

```csharp
ArenaBuilder.Asset<ChartStop>("Rootmarket",s=>{s.id="Rootmarket";s.displayName="Rootmarket";s.region="Xylos";s.blurb="The river town on the roots: Hadda's and Binna's stalls, the Press and the mender's cradle.";
    s.zone="Rootmarket";s.spawn="Arrival";s.position=new Vector2(.52f,.66f);s.routes=new[]{"TallowDrift"};s.shop=s.bench=s.repair=true;s.chapter=3;
    s.reaches=new[]{"XylosNearspace","BraidedRiver","WalkingCanopy"};});
```

| Field | Use |
|---|---|
| `id` | the asset's file name; a route names the stop by it (`Stop_Rootmarket`). One stop per zone |
| `displayName`, `region`, `blurb` | what a player reads: the place, where it is, one line on why anyone goes back (it has two lines of the page at size 24). All three required |
| `zone`, `spawn` | where travelling arrives: a `ZoneDef` and a `SpawnPoint` id that really is in its scene (default `Arrival`). Pick the spawn in the settlement itself, clear of any fight |
| `unlockFlag` | the flag that says the party has stood there. **Leave it empty**: it is then `visited.<zone>`, which the game sets itself the moment any zone of chapter 3 or later is entered (`ArenaRuntime` -> `RouteChart.Arrive`, behind the load's black, so the arrival's autosave holds it). Until it is set the stop shows as "Uncharted", nameless, and can't be chosen |
| `lockedFlag`, `lockedReason` | a story beat that closes the place: while the flag is set the stop keeps its name, shows CLOSED and refuses with `lockedReason`, which must say what's happened in a player's words. Clear the flag and it opens again |
| `position` | where its lantern stands, x and y each 0 to 1: x west to east, y south to north (below) |
| `routes` | ids of the stops a route line joins it to: the one the party first came here from. Lit between two charted stops, faint toward an uncharted one |
| `shop`, `bench`, `repair` | what really stands in the settlement (the page lists them; a test loads the zone and looks for the `Bench` and the `RepairBay`) |
| `chapter` | 3 to 10: the chart's order, and `DevLoadout chapter<N>` charts every stop of an earlier chapter |
| `reaches` | the zones reached from here and nowhere else (its approach, its wilds, its dungeon). While the party is in one, the party's arrow stands at this stop; while the open main quest's step lies in one, so does the story's diamond |

- **The visited rule.** Chapters 1 and 2's zones write no flag (their saves and tests are byte for byte what they were). Their four stops (`Decks`, `SorrelOutpost`, `TallowDrift`, `Lanternwick`, made by `ContentScaffold.Stamp` because their builders can't be rerun) name the story flag only someone standing there could have set and the story can't reach the chart without: `met.Orrin`, `met.Survivor`, `met.Keeper`, `met.Aumi`. So a save from before the chart existed has all four, with no repair. Don't name a flag on a later stop unless the place truly opens on something other than arriving.
- **Position.** Lanterns stand in a field 980 by 300 at 1080p with a name under each, so two stops must differ by **.23 in x or .29 in y** (`RouteChart.ApartX/ApartY`), and every stop must be reachable from every other by d-pad (a press goes to the nearest stop in that direction, no more than twice as far aside as ahead). The lint and `RouteChartTests` check both for the stops that exist, so check yours against those of the chapters before. The house grid is five columns and three rows, clean at every chapter along the way; nudge by .02 so it doesn't look ruled:

  | x | taken | free |
  |---|---|---|
  | .03 | Decks (.03,.56), Sorrel Outpost (.05,.12) | (.03,1) |
  | .28 | Tallow Drift (.27,.88), Lanternwick (.29,.38) | (.28,0) |
  | .52 | Rootmarket (.52,.66) | (.52,.14) chapter 4, (.52,1) |
  | .76 | | (.76,.42) chapter 5, (.76,.92) chapter 6, (.76,0) chapter 7 |
  | 1 | | (1,.66) chapter 8, (1,.2) chapter 9, (1,.98) |
- **The objective mark** is worked out, not written: the stop whose `zone` or `reaches` holds the open main quest's step, by `stepTargets[step].scene`, or the target of a `Reach` step. A step that names no scene marks nothing, so give main-quest steps their `scene` (section 8).
- **Travel** is offered from any zone to any charted stop but the one the party is in, asks first ("Travel — Rootmarket"), and goes by `SceneFlow.LoadZone(zone,spawn)` like a warp: it autosaves on arrival and the zone gives the form. It is refused, with the reason on the page, in a boss fight (`BossController.AnyFightUnderway`), with an enemy awake within 15 m of the hero (`EnemyBrain.AwakeNear(...,InteractionCalm)`), in a conversation, and in a `SpaceCombat` zone while any encounter is begun and not cleared. ### 4.3 Shader

Everything in the world draws with `Lattice/Toon` (`A/Art/Shaders/LatticeToon.shader`). Always on: `_BaseMap`, `_BaseColor`, `_RampSteps` (2-6), `_ShadowTint`, rim, `_LightFloor`, `_EmissionMap` x `_EmissionColor`. Toggles, each a `shader_feature_local` keyword:

| Keyword | Does |
|---|---|
| `_VCOLOR_ON` | multiplies vertex colour into albedo (floor tones, dark wall tops) |
| `_TRIPLANAR_ON` | steep faces sample the base map from the side |
| `_STRATA_ON` | horizontal rock bands by world height, with a slope mask |
| `_DETAIL_ON` | world-space macro and micro noise on ground |
| `_OCCFADE_ON` | screen-door fade of whatever hides the hero |
| `_EDGEFADE_ON` | soft alpha edges on patches and paths |
| `_PANELS_ON` | world-projected hull seams |
| `_WATER_ON`, `_ICE_ON`, `_GEL_ON` | depth-tinted water with foam; ice; jelly |
| `_WIND_ON` | vertex sway |
| `_GLOW_ON`, `_SURF_ON`, `_DISSOLVE_ON`, `_ALPHATEST_ON` | unlit glow; UV-free fur/chitin/stone/mech detail; KO dissolve; clip |

**A variant no material in the build enables is stripped from the player.** A keyword first switched on at runtime works in the editor and does nothing in the build. Other shaders: `Lattice/DriftingSky`, `Lattice/Telegraph`, `Lattice/TelegraphFill`, `Lattice/SignPlate`, `Lattice/EnergyBubble`, `Lattice/FlightEnergy`, `Lattice/ShieldPlate` (a guardian's plate) (`A/Resources/Effects/`). A property block isn't saved with a scene: anything set that way is applied by a component at load (`PropTint`, `StaysDark`).

**An editor still never runs a particle system**, so a particle's look is only seen in play. A particle is its texture drawn on a square: the texture must come to nothing at every edge or the square shows (the Frozen Host's vent steam drew stacked white squares with the light curtain's `curtain_soft.png`, which is solid to its sides; whole-game capture s35/097). `ZoneKit.Drift` gives a strand's glow the round `puff_soft.png` (`ParticleSprites`) draws it as vapour (over what is behind it, never added light) and thins vapour packed so tight that a line of sight meets more than .6 of it (`DRIFT_THINNED`); `flare_01` is a pinpoint spark, not a body or a puff (a swarm's motes draw their own). `EffectsBuildTests` holds every particle the build draws to a round sprite, every `Shader.Find` and `Resources.Load` name in the game's scripts to something the build carries, and every material made at run time to a shader, keyword set and textures the build carries. A struck body's flash (`_HitFlash`) lifts its light and burns at its silhouette; it is never a fill.

### 4.4 Ordering and intake of generated art

Visible art is generated, not sourced (`docs/ASSET_SOURCES.md`); packs are only stand-ins, rig donors, particles, fonts and sound. Procedure (`docs/art/PIPELINE.md`; chapter 2's scripts are the model):

1. **Order** [art runner]. Copy `tools/meshy/c9_lantern_batch.py`: stages `refs` (design boards, portrait atlases, tiling textures), `isolate` (cut boards into single objects with `tools/isolate_board.py`), `models` (`meshy.py img3d --band prop --target-polycount 6000`), `reskin` (`meshy.py retexture` of an existing rigged body, original UVs). Chapter 3's `tools/meshy/c10_xylos_batch.py` already follows it. Parallel tasks used to race on the ledger `docs/art/gen-manifest.json` and drop rows, hence NEXT_SESSION_PROMPT's "one at a time"; since `19638a0` `meshy.py` takes a `manifest_lock` around every write and says tasks may run side by side. `meshy.py` refuses unless `docs/art/species-approval.json` is approved and stops at a credit cap. Review every isolated reference before spending on 3D. Output lands in git-ignored `art-src/Generated/<batch>/`.
2. **Prepare rows** [art runner]. Copy `tools/c9_intake.py`: for a static prop it strips embedded textures in Blender (`_clean.fbx`), keeps an albedo of at most 1024 px, derives an emission mask (`tools/emission_mask.py`) and upserts a row in **`docs/art/intake.json`** (the file `GenIntake` reads; `gen-manifest.json` is only the ledger). For a reskin it copies the source id's row (model, clips) and swaps the textures.

```json
{"id":"LanternTower","model":"art-src/Generated/C9-Lantern/LanternTower_clean.fbx","albedo":"art-src/Generated/C9-Lantern/LanternTower_albedo.png","emission":"art-src/Generated/C9-Lantern/LanternTower_emission.png","emissionColor":"#FFB62E","kind":"static","folder":"Environment","size":12,"fit":"height","yaw":0,"clips":[]}
```

   Row fields: `kind` `static|biped|generic`; `folder` `Environment|Enemies|Characters|Ships`; `fit` `height|length|depth` with `size` in metres; `enemy` (binds `EnemyDef.prefab`, so **the EnemyDef must exist first**) or `character`+`form` (binds a `CharacterDef` body); `clips` (`state`, `path`, `name`; a rigged row needs `Idle` and `Walk`; humanoid donors are in `A/Art/Animation/UAL-Skeleton.fbx`, quadruped in `Fox-*.anim`).
3. **Intake** [lead]. `GenIntake.BuildPrefix` with `-intake-prefix <id prefixes>`, marker `GEN_INTAKE_OK`. It stages to `A/Art/Generated/Models/<id>/`, makes `<id>_Toon.mat`, normalises scale and pivot (feet at the origin), and writes `A/Prefabs/<folder>/<id>.prefab`. It adds no colliders. The prefix is `StartsWith`: `Muster` matches every `Muster*` row.
4. **Use.** The key is now live; rerun the zone build.

Traps: a bad `emissionColor` gives black emission silently; an unknown `kind` or `fit` silently means static or height; a pitch on an already-upright model lays it flat and scales it by the wrong axis (D213); a retexture cannot fix a shape (D214: a new body for a named person, not a new coat); a flat-colour texture reads as an untextured slab (`CrystalClusterA`'s glass).

### Enemy behaviours

Past the six archetypes, an enemy fights by a **behaviour**: one of eight families in `S/Combat/Enemy<Family>.cs`, picked by `EnemyDef.behaviour` (`EnemyBehaviourKind`, append only) and tuned by `EnemyDef.tune` (`BehaviourTune`) and the definition's `attacks`. No family names an enemy; a later chapter's enemy is a definition. Written with chapter 3 (uncommitted and not yet run through the whole suite when this was written; chapter 3's roster in `S/Editor/World/XylosBuilder.Enemies.cs` is the worked example and `T/PlayMode/XylosCombatTests.cs` has one test for each family).

**Archetype and behaviour are two questions.** `archetype` still says what kind of body it is to the party's skills and the camera: a `Sentinel` is too heavy to reel and a hook pulls the hero to it, a `Mine` holds its ground, a `PackHunter` or `Swarm` can be shoved, a `Spitter` is framed as a shooter. `behaviour` says how it attacks. `None` fights by archetype alone, as the first fourteen do, and nothing about them changed.

**What every family keeps.** A behaviour drives the brain's own tell (`EnemyBrain.BeginTell`), so `Telegraphing`, `AttackMark`, `TellDuration` and `TelegraphRemaining` mean what they always did to the partner, the replay driver, a rigged body's Attack clip, a rigid body's wind-up (`EnemyTellPose`, which a behaviour can give its own pitch, roll and swell) and the shared tests. A hit lands only inside a marking that stood at least half a second, and stops at solid cover (D207). What an attack leaves on the floor is a `LingeringArea`: a boundary marking (pale, under alpha .99) unless it damages, when it is drawn as a danger (D217, D222), and its marking is owned by the area, never by the body, so nothing reads a cloud as a blow about to land. A sound that isn't the tell is played from the drawn body or from nothing (`EnemyBehaviour.Sound`), because `EveryOrdinaryEnemysWarningSoundHasAMarkOnTheGround` holds every cue from the body's root to having a marking under it.

## 6. A new boss

A boss is an `EnemyDef` with `boss=true`: `ActorFactory` adds `BossController`, which runs marked specials between the brain's ordinary attack.

1. **Definition.** Integrity is sized so the replay party takes 120 to 300 s (D206; chapter values 35000 to 55000; aim for about three minutes), measured as `QaPlanCombatTests.ABossLastsTwoToFiveMinutesAtItsExpectedSync` measures it: a boss since chapter 2 against its own chapter's party (`DevLoadout chapter<N>`, N its zone's chapter, at its sheet's Sync), chapters 1 and 2's four in Refined weapons as they always were. A boss wider than half its height needs `capsuleHeight` at least twice its Kit's radius, or Unity stretches the capsule and the boss stands off its floor (`XylosCombatTests.EveryBossSinceTheSecondChapterStandsOnTheFloorItFightsOn`). Its three weak spots (children `WeakPoint_0` to `_2`, double damage) are placed by `BossController.WeakSpotAt`: on a body wider than 1.4 m they stand .2 m proud of the capsule at 1.4 m up, ahead and on both flanks; chapters 1 and 2's four keep the places they always had (the flanks proud, the middle one inside the body). A Kit that wants them elsewhere sets `weakSpots` (local positions); a Kit that hides them (Dessik Varn's cockpit) hides the children by that name. `QaPlanCombatTests.EveryBossHasAWeakSpotABladeCanReachFromTheEdgeOfItsBody` requires every spot of a boss since chapter 2 to be reachable by a cut from a hero standing at the capsule's edge. `breakThreshold` 600 to 900, `yieldsOnDefeat=true` for a person who surrenders. Retune in the builder: chapter 2's tests read the builder's source and require the literal `e.integrity=<n>;` to equal the asset (`QaFairTests.cs`, `QaFightLookTests.cs`), and a new boss should get the same pin.
2. **Moves**: `S/Combat/BossController.<Name>.cs`, a `partial class BossController` (model: `BossController.Hessa.cs`). A chooser and one coroutine per move:

### 6.1 Difficulty: nothing to add per chapter

A player picks **Gentle, Standard or Demanding**: asked once at New Game, changed at any time in the pause menu's Settings. Standard is the game as tuned: every number in this guide, every test and every route. The other two change two numbers and nothing else (no tell, window, XP, scrip or loot). Type-checked only when this was written; `T/PlayMode/DifficultyTests.cs` has not been run.

| | An enemy's or a boss's hit on the party | Integrity an enemy or a boss is made with |
|---|---|---|
| Gentle | 60% | 80% |
| Demanding | 130% | 115% |

- **Damage is taken where the hit lands**: `Health.Receive`, for any packet whose `source` is an enemy's `Health` (`S/Core/Difficulty.cs`, `Difficulty.EnemyDamage`). `EnemyBrain`'s attacks, a shot already in the air, `BossController.Packet(...)`, a summon and a packet a boss file builds by hand are all covered with nothing written per enemy or boss, and a boss's phase scaling (`brain.DamageScale`) is left as it is. **Give every enemy packet its `source`.** A packet with none is the place's damage (`PressurePulse`, `RisingWater`, a wall flown into) and is left alone on all three, on purpose.
- **Integrity is taken once, as the body is made**: `ActorFactory.Enemy` (`Difficulty.EnemyIntegrity`). A body that sets its own `Health.maximum` afterwards asks the same call (the Cantor's collar does, for its head and links). A `Health` a builder puts in a scene (a pressure organ, a seed pod) is not an enemy and keeps its number. Changed mid-fight, the next hit follows at once and whoever is awake keeps their bar. A boss on show before its fight (`previewPoint`) has no bar yet and is made again at the new setting (`Spawner` listens to `Difficulty.Changed`), so don't keep a reference to a previewed body: ask for it when it is needed. A Retry reloads the zone and keeps the player's setting.
- **Tune and test on Standard.** The setting lives in the save (`GameState.difficulty`) and nowhere else, so a fixture's state, `GameServices.NewGame()`, `title.StartGame(...)` and a save written before the setting are all Standard, and a test does nothing to get it. A test about the setting sets `GameServices.Current.State.difficulty` and never leaves anything but Standard in the game's own save slots.
- **Routes run Standard whatever save they continue.** The driver holds the route's `"difficulty"` every frame: `"Gentle"`, `"Standard"` or `"Demanding"`, and Standard when the route leaves it out. `route_lint.py` rejects any other value; in a generator it is `r.difficulty = "Gentle"` before `r.write()`. The New Game question is never shown under the driver, in the editor, in batch mode or with a development argument (`DifficultyUi.AsksAtNewGame`), so a route presses through the title exactly as before.
- **Nothing shows the setting during play**: no mark on the HUD, no word on a card. Don't add one, and where a screen has to name the three, use `Difficulty.Name` and `Difficulty.Says`, which describe them by their numbers.

### 7.1 A named NPC

1. **CharacterDef** in `Define()`: `Person("Aumi","Keeper Aumi",true,"AumiBody","Characters","Neve")` sets `id=speakerId`, `displayName`, `female` and the `natural` body (with a fallback body until the reskin lands). The id is the Yarn speaker and the save key; `displayName` is what a player reads.
2. **Portrait.** One 4x4 sheet of sixteen expressions in `PortraitEmotion` order (`Neutral, Happy, Sad, Angry, Shocked, Worried, Intense, Synced, Amused, Smug, Curious, Flustered, Tender, Determined, Playful, Special`; append-only). `python tools/portrait_pipeline.py --sheet <png> --name <Id>_Natural --order 0,1,...,15` slices, upscales and assembles `art-src/Generated/P3/portraits/<name>/<name>.png` (2304x2304); `--order` is the reviewed source cell for each expression, never guessed. Then `PortraitIntake.ImportNamed` with `-portrait-names <Id>_Natural,...` writes the sliced sheet and `A/Resources/Portraits/<Id>_Natural.asset` and binds `CharacterDef.portraitNatural`. A missing portrait hides the picture silently; a missing expression falls back to Neutral silently; the tests don't.
3. **Place** with `Person(id,at,yaw,postFlag,post,finalFlag,finalNode)`, which wraps `WorldBuilder.Npc`: nodes `<Id>First`, `<Id>Repeat`, `<Id>Post`, prompt "Talk to <display name>", range 3.2, a capsule, a name label. `Npc`'s own default `postFlag` is `"warpkey"`, so always set it.
4. **Which node plays** (`Npc.Interact`): a `holdNode`; else a side-story beat (`SideStories.NodeFor`); else the first meeting while `met.<Id>` is unset; else `finalNode` once `finalFlag` is set; else `postNode` once `postFlag` is set; else `repeatNode`. A finished conversation reports `TalkTo <speaker>` to the quests.
5. **Post-states.** Different words: the tiers above, or a beat. Gone or arrived: a `FlagGate` (Pell leaves when `pell.aboard` is set; her lamp lights on `pell.lampReturned`). Decided once at load: a small component (`YieldedCaptain`).

### 7.2 Dialogue

Source is `docs/writing/<Region>.yarn`; the builder's `Dialogue` entry copies every `.yarn` and `barks.json` into `Resources` and reimports `Lattice.yarnproject` (which globs `**/*.yarn`), then checks a hard-coded list of node names. **A new `.yarn` file is not in the compiled program until that runs, and a missing node fails silently at runtime.** Give your builder its own `RefreshDialogue` list.

```

-> Show us what you have.
    <<shop>>

    Corra: Later's fine. Later still pays. #emotion:Smug

```

- Every line is `Speaker: text #emotion:Name`. The speaker is a `CharacterDef` id (`Keeper:` shows as "Keeper Abri"). An unknown emotion logs and plays Neutral.
- Commands (`S/Dialogue/DialogueSystem.cs`): `<<setFlag key>>` (also reports a `Flag` objective; a key starting `chapter` autosaves when the talk closes), `<<startQuest Id>>`, `<<giveItem Id n>>`, `<<shop>>`, `<<repair>>`, `<<bench>>`, `<<joinParty Id>>`, `<<warpUnlock>>`, `<<stationShift>>`. Flags, quests and items take effect the moment the command is reached; a shop, repair, bench or party join waits for the conversation to finish, and an interrupted one drops them.
- A node said straight after another in the same box (the box stays up and the party held between them): `Npc.SayNext(node,speaker,done)` while the first runs (`DialogueSystem.Follow`, November 14, for the Coronach's extras). Refused with nothing running; dropped with a talk cut short.
- A line said by nobody present: `DiscoveryPoint` with a `dialogueNode` (Sela at a relay). On arrival or in a fight: a bark (`barks.json`, `{"Speaker":{"situation":"line|line"}}`; `zone` and `zone.flight` on arrival).
- Words: no ids, no camel case, no `TODO`. The player-text check rejects any word shaped like `aB...C` or `word.word`, and a list of retired phrases. The bare substring `bridge` is banned only in chapters 1 and 2's nine script files (it was the Decks' walkway's retired name); a script written since may say it.
- Compulsory conversations stay under 90 s at reading pace. Replay routes press through a conversation line by line, so changing a node's length changes its route (section 10).

### 7.3 Side-story beats

`SideStories.Beats` (`S/World/SideStories.cs`) is an ordered list of `new("Speaker","Node",state=>condition)`; the first that matches plays instead of the speaker's tier, so order is priority. Write each so it can only be true once its speaker has been met (`Met(s,"Pell")`, or a quest step that implies it): a beat must never preempt a first meeting. Offers, returns and afterwords all go here:

```csharp
new("Pell","PellLampReturn",s=>Step(s,"PellsLamp")==1),
new("Pell","PellLampOffer",s=>Step(s,"PellsLamp")<0&&Met(s,"Pell")&&Step(s,"LanternRoad")>=3),
new("Pell","PellLampAfter",s=>Flag(s,"pell.lampReturned")&&!Flag(s,"lanternwick.chartGiven")),
```

A beat that stays true after it has been heard (a repeat line) must be added to `PeopleMarks.Repeats`, or its speaker wears a "!" forever.

### 7.4 Townsfolk

Unnamed residents are props with a body (LRB `Townsfolk`, `Townsperson`): an existing character prefab as `"Generated civilian"`, a `PropTint` so nobody takes one for the person whose body it borrows, a static `BoxCollider` (.7 x 1.9 x .7) so the bake walks round them, animators set to `CullCompletely`. Keep them off every route, off signs, and out of the camera's line to anyone with a name; the frame can hold about four animated bodies. A walking civilian is `ScriptedWalk` (chapter 1's market morning; staged by `MarketOpeningStaging`). The first two chapters' towns (the Decks, Tallow Drift, Lanternwick) get theirs from `S/Editor/World/TownLife*.cs` (`TownLife.Apply` stages the Decks and Tallow; `LanternRoadBuilder` calls it for Lanternwick), and its `Audit` is the whole rule set in one place: two metres off every replay route and story walk, out of the camera's line to the named and to prompts, off signs, out of doorways, not inside a prop, no neighbour in the same body, and an errand's line clear of anything solid. An unprofiled walker holds its soles only at .7 of its body's measured walk (`CivilianStride.Measure`), so `TownLife.Pace` walks each errand at that.

**Looks (November 21).** Every scene under `A/Scenes` is dressed as it is saved, whoever saves it (`S/Editor/World/TownsfolkLooks.cs`, a `sceneSaving` hook): its ordinary townspeople are given looks so that no two who can share one frame of the play camera read as one figure twice. The bodies are a dozen species meshes repainted per named person on one UV layout, so one mesh is one *template*, and two of one template are copies unless at least three of their clothes, head, what they carry and their build differ (`S/World/TownsfolkLook.cs`, shared with the test; a tint is not a look). A builder still chooses who lives where and in whose body; the pass keeps that painting where it can and reaches, cheapest first, for one of the body's four dyes (`tools/townsfolk_dyes.py`: clothes and hair redyed on its own UVs, `A/Art/Generated/Townsfolk/<Body>`), another painting of the template the place already has, a cap, band or scarf (`Worn …`), a pack, satchel, bedroll, coil or parcel (`Carried …`; a builder's own `Carried …` is kept) and a build (8% in height, 12% in breadth; an errand walked at its height's pace). Nobody moves and nothing solid is added. A body in a force's colours is a uniform (`TownsfolkLook.Uniforms`) and is left as it is. What the builder gave stays on each person (`TownsfolkDress`), so saving again gives the same people. A new body for townsfolk: run the dye tool for it, then `TownsfolkLooks.Make`. `TownsfolkLookTests` holds every zone to it, along the routes and at every resident, and reports each zone as built and as dressed.

**Seats (November 21).** The same save fits every body a `Posed` holds seated to its seat (`S/Editor/World/Seating.cs`): the one sitting pose leaves the knees about .29 m up whatever the legs, so each sitter is lifted until their soles rest on the floor they were placed on (onto the bench, feet hanging, where the bench is taller than their pelvis), and where their pelvis then stands over what is under it they get a seat of their own with its top at the pelvis's underside: a cushion or a folded blanket on a bench, a crate on the bare floor (`Seat …`). Place a seated body on the floor it sits over and give it the seat height you mean (`Posed.seat`); the rule does the rest, keeps the facing and `Posed.SeatTurn`, and records what it did (`SeatFit`). `SeatedTests` (EditMode, one case per zone) holds every seated body's soles to the floor and its pelvis on, not in, its seat (`S/World/SeatGeometry.cs`, shared).

## 8. Quests

`QuestDef` (`S/Data/QuestDef.cs`): `id`, `title`, `description`, `steps[]` (`kind`, `target`, `count`), `stepText[]` (one per step, never blank), `rewardXp`, `rewardScrip`, `rewards[]` (`Ingredient{id,count}`), `flagsOnComplete[]`; and for the story and the objective panel `main`, `chapter`, `stepTargets[]`, `closingLine`, `next`, `storyFlags[]`, and for the chapter's cards `cardFlag`, `cardBody` (below).

| Kind | `target` | What reports it |
|---|---|---|
| `Reach` | scene name | `ArenaRuntime` on every zone load |
| `TalkTo` | speaker id | a conversation that finishes |
| `Kill` | enemy id | `LootDrop` on death |
| `Collect` | material id | `Inventory.Grant` |
| `Interact` | a `DiscoveryPoint.report` string; `count` points share it | `DiscoveryPoint` when taken |
| `Flag` | a flag key | **only** `<<setFlag>>` in Yarn (a flag set by `FlagAll` or code doesn't advance a step until the next load) |


- **Main quest.** Starts from a conversation (`<<startQuest LanternRoad>>` in `AbriLanternTale`), walks `Reach`/`TalkTo`/`Interact`/`Kill`, ends on a `Flag` set by the closing talk, and sets `chapter<N>.complete`. Mark it on the definition and it is the story everywhere: `q.main=true;q.chapter=3;`. The quest log leads with it (`PauseMenu.MainQuests` is read from the definitions through `StoryQuests`), the side-quest rules and tests leave it out, and **its current step is the objective panel's line**, in every zone, old ones included, from the moment it starts: nothing is written in `ObjectiveHud` (its `switch` is chapters 1 and 2's and takes no new cases). The words are `stepText[step]`; keep them to what the panel holds in two lines (about 80 characters).
  - **Where the arrow points** is worked out from the step itself: a `Reach` step points at the way out that leads to its scene; `TalkTo` at that speaker; `Interact` at the nearest discovery still to make with that `report`; `Kill` at the boss once its bar is up, before that at the uncleared encounter that holds the enemy. `stepTargets[step]` (optional, one per step when given) says more:

    | `QuestTarget` field | Use |
    |---|---|
    | `scene`, `via[]` | where the step happens. From any other zone the arrow goes to the way out that leads there, or failing that toward the first scene of `via` this zone has a way to (the scenes between, nearest the goal first) |
    | `speaker`, `report`, `encounter` | in the scene: that `Npc`, the nearest wanted `DiscoveryPoint` with this `report`, the `EncounterVolume` with this id |
    | `hasPoint`, `point` | a world point, when none of those is there to find |
    | `brief` | a side quest's step in about 40 characters, for the dim second line |

    ```csharp
    q.stepTargets=new[]{new QuestTarget{scene="XylosNearspace",via=new[]{"TallowApproach"}},new QuestTarget{scene="XylosNearspace",report="XylosBarge"},new QuestTarget{scene="Rootmarket",speaker="Ruva",via=new[]{"XylosNearspace"}}};
    ```
  - **Between chapters.** `q.closingLine` is what the panel says once this quest is done and no later story quest is open; empty shows the zone's name. It ends with what to do next, in about a hundred characters ("…: read the chart for Hoarfell at the chart table"), and `q.next` (a `QuestTarget`, as a step's) is where it sends the party: the place the next chapter begins, with `via` for the way there from wherever this chapter ends. The panel's arrow and the chart's diamond follow it (D273). The chapter that begins there writes both onto the quest before it, and adds its hand-off to `ContentFrameworkTests.HandOffs`: the chapter told, each zone a party could be standing in, and what the arrow must find there. Chapter 4's first closing line was "Many Hands is waiting", read on a bridge three zones from the dock it meant.
  - **The chapter's cards** come from the main quest too (`S/UI/ChapterCards.cs`, drawn by `QuestToast`); nothing is written in either file. Every main quest past the first three **opens** its chapter on a short plate across the foot of the screen, `CHAPTER <chapter>` over its `title` in capitals, for 4.5 s, ahead of its own NEW QUEST card; and **ends** it on `CHAPTER <chapter> COMPLETE` over the same title. Two optional fields say when and with what words:

    | Field | Use |
    |---|---|
    | `q.cardFlag` | the flag that brings the closing card up when it turns true in play: the closing conversation's (`q.cardFlag="chapter3.complete";`, as chapter 2's is `chapter2.homecoming`). Empty: the quest's own completion, which is too early for a chapter whose last talk comes after its quest is paid |
    | `q.cardBody` | the words under the title, held nine seconds: two lines of the plate, about 190 characters (chapter 2's are 193). Empty: the heading and title alone, on the short plate, for 4.5 s; `closingLine` is not repeated on the card |

    Both cards wait for the conversation to close, show once, and never on a load; one conversation that ends a chapter and begins the next says the ending first. `DevLoadout chapter<N>` sets each earlier chapter's `cardFlag`. Keep to **one main quest a chapter**: each brings both cards. Chapter 2's card stays written out in `QuestToast` (`ChapterEnd*`) whatever `LanternRoad.asset` says, and chapters 1 and 2 open on no card, as they shipped. The game's last chapter ends on the credits instead (D288): a main quest whose `cardFlag` is `game.complete` (`ChapterCards.EndsOnCredits`) opens on its card and never closes on one; `S/UI/Credits.cs` rolls in its place. `T/PlayMode/ChapterCardTests.cs` puts every later main quest's cards up on the real plates and fails by itself on a title or body that overflows, a main quest with no `chapter`, or two in one chapter.
  - **`q.storyFlags`** lists every flag the chapter's story has set by its end that the steps and `flagsOnComplete` don't already say (relays lit, doors opened). Nothing in play reads it; `DevLoadout chapter<N>` does (section 11).
- **Side quest (D199).** An offer node with `<<startQuest>>`, a thing in the world, a return node with `<<setFlag>>`. Steps advance on flags the side conversations set, never on `TalkTo`, so the main story's talk with the same person can't tick them. Exactly one node in the whole script starts each quest.
- **A thing to take** is `Discovery(name,at,prompt,flag,report,node,speaker,quest,step)` with `quest`/`step` set: offered only while that quest is at that step. Give it a `visual` (shown only while it can be taken) and switch the visual off in the builder. Make it look unlike its surroundings (the ration crate is the one pale crate, under a light).
- **Briefs.** A side step's line under the objective comes from the definition: `q.stepTargets[step].brief` (about 40 characters) and `.speaker` (who it waits on, which also puts the dim arrow on them). Without a brief the full `stepText` is used and must still fit one line at size 24 (a test measures it). `QuestText.Briefs` is the first ten quests' table and takes no additions.
- **Completion** pays once: scrip, XP to each member, rewards, `quest.<id>.complete`, `flagsOnComplete`.
- **Balance (D208).** 125 XP a level; a test sums every side quest's `rewardXp` into the Sync band. New quests move that sum.

Hand-written definition assets also exist (`QuestDef/NeveParcel.asset` is YAML written by hand with its own `.meta`). Prefer `Define()`: it needs no GUIDs and survives review as code.

## 10. Replay routes

The driver (`S/UI/ContinuousReview.cs`, schema `S/UI/QualityRoute.cs`) plays a route JSON with a virtual pad: real inputs, no invulnerability, and no pathfinding but the baked floor's own way to a fallen hero and on a walk that asks for it (`findWay`). A step whose only button is `North` (Swap) and which names an `expectedCharacter` means "take the lead as them": the driver presses only while somebody else leads, because who leads at a load depends on how the fights before it went (a blind press handed the lead away once Taren stopped falling in chapter 1's haul). A route is a list of flat steps, each: `name`, `seconds` (at most 180), held `buttons` / stick `x`,`y` / triggers, optional steering (`navigate` + `point` + `tolerance`, `findWay`, `approachTarget`, `approachPartner`), reactions (`evadeTelegraphs`, `evadeProjectiles`), `pickRow` for menus, an end condition (`until`, required; `stopWhen`, not required: `arrived`, `targetDown`, `partyAlive`, `stocked`, `saved`, `flag:<key>`, `ui:<state>`, `scene:<zone>`, `row:<name>`) and post-conditions (`expectedScene`, `expectedFlag`, `expectedUi`, `expectedForm`, `expectedCharacter`). It parses with `JsonUtility`, so a misspelt key is ignored without a word by the driver, which is why **every route goes through `tools/route_lint.py`**: it rejects a key that isn't a field of `QualityRoute`/`QualityStep`/`QualitySegment` (read from `QualityRoute.cs` itself), a value of the wrong type, a scene that isn't `Title` or a `ZoneDef` asset's file name (the route's, `expectedScene`, `scene:<zone>`), an `until`/`stopWhen` form the driver doesn't know, unknown UI states, forms, heroes, buttons and loadouts, and the knobs of the driver's own hands (below) asked for in words it doesn't know. `python tools/route_lint.py` checks every route in `docs/quality/routes` (`ROUTE_LINT_OK`); `tools/quality_route.py`, the preflight every replay runs, calls it too. `--scenes A,B` accepts zones whose `ZoneDef` doesn't exist yet.

Chapter routes are generated: **edit the generator, never the JSON.** Copy `tools/lantern_routes.py` (import its `Route` class; generation is under `__main__`):

```python
def gorge():                                                                  # abridged from the real one
    r = Route("chapter-14-lantern-gorge", "Chapter 2: three relays, the den, the camp, the door")
    r.resume("LanternGorge", "met.Aumi", speed=COMBAT_SPEED, spawn=(0, 0))    # Title, Continue, pick the autosave, assert flag and scene
    r.lead("Taren")
    r.walk("into the gorge", 2, 0, 24)                                        # a straight line to x, y, z
    r.fight_ground("Gorge_0")                                                 # lock, skills, chase, dodge (the driver mends: autoHeal); ends on flag:clear.Gorge_0
    r.walk("to the first relay", 8.2, 0.2, 50.5, tolerance=0.5)
    r.talk("first gorge relay", 3, flag="gorge.relay1")                       # presses through 3 lines, waits for ui:world, asserts the flag
    r.walk("to Pell's lamp", -24.5, 0, 99.5, tolerance=1)
    r.add("pick up the chewed lamp", 0.3, buttons=["South"], expectedFlag="pell.lampFound")
    r.add("release lamp", 0.5)
    r.walk("to the buried door", 0, 0.2, 226.8, tolerance=0.5)
    r.scene = "SunkenRelay"                                                   # set before the step that changes zone
    r.add("enter the Sunken Relay", 4, buttons=["South"], expectedUi="world", expectedForm="Shaped")
    r.write()
```

A boss is written inline: `r.door(...)` onto its floor, the lock, then rounds that `stopWhen "flag:bossdown.<Id>"` (`relay()`, Hessa). Only chapters 12 to 16 have a generator: `chapter-01` to `chapter-11` are hand-kept JSON, edited in place.
- **The driver's own hands.** Four knobs, each off unless the route says so, each done with the pad and nothing else (`S/UI/ReplayHands.cs` decides, `ContinuousReview` presses; `T/PlayMode/ReplayHandsTests.cs`), and a fifth, `wearBest`, done with the Gear page's own call. Nobody writes a gel press into a fight any more.
  - `"autoHeal": true` (route; `Route.auto_heal`, on by default in the generator). On foot in a combat zone the driver looks after the lead's Integrity: in a fight, under half, it cycles the quick item (d-pad) to the mend that fits what is missing and presses LT, never inside the game's 4 s wait between items; between fights it tops up under three quarters with an item none of which is wasted; with the partner down it uses a Relink Spike, or with none walks to them and stands by them (in any step that isn't a walk to a point). In flight it does nothing: LT is the brake and the game has no quick item there. A route about a defeat leaves it off (`chapter-03-defeat-retry`).

The driver presses the step's button until the party carries that many or a press buys nothing, and `"until":"stocked"` ends the step; what went in is in `run.json` (`stocks`) and the log (`QUALITY_STOCK`). `r.stock("buy repair gel up to the cap", "Repair Gel")`; at a bench add `ui="bench", pauseUi="bench"`.   - **Never press Swap once and expect "the other hero".** Say who leads: `r.lead("Taren")` (`"ensureCharacter"`). The driver presses Swap, half a second apart, until that hero leads. It gives up after six presses (`ContinuousReview.EnsureSwaps`) and at once for a hero who is resting where the pair can't be changed or for a name the party hasn't got: the step ends there and fails with its reason ("Sela is down", "Idra is resting, and the party can only be changed...", "nobody called..."), instead of pressing until the seconds ran out. The older builders (`gullet_routes.py`, `motion_routes.py`, `quality_routes.py`, `station_routes.py`) still press Swap once in the routes they have always written, which are evidence and stay as they are; each has a `lead(hero)` beside them for anything new.
  - **Bringing a rester out** is done with the pad, where a player could: `r.deploy("Idra")` in a safe zone. Without `pauseUi` the driver won't open a menu (the run would be rejected as "simulation paused") and fails the step saying so. Either way it leaves the menu closed. At a bench in a wild zone, with the bench already open and `"pauseUi":"bench"` on the step, the same step is written to turn to the Party page with LB and do the rest; only the safe-zone way has been played by a test (`PartyOfThreeTests`), so look at the first route that uses a bench, or write it out: turn to the Party page, `"pickRow":"Hero_Idra"`, press.

With a `loadout` the driver puts that pair out with the loadout; continuing a save it checks the pair at the first step with a party and fails the run there, naming both pairs, if the save has another one out. The lint wants two different heroes, each a `CharacterDef`; `--heroes Idra` accepts one whose asset Unity hasn't written yet.
  - `partnerDown`, `partyAlive` and `approachPartner` go by role (`PartyController.Partner`: the hero out beside the lead, the fallen one first), not by a place in an array; `rangedTolerance` applies to a lead who shoots on foot (today that is Sela: `ContinuousReview.ShootsOnFoot`).
- **Travel by Sela's chart** (section 3.10): `r.chart("Lanternwick", "Lanternwick", spawn=(0, -14), form="Natural")`, the stop's `ChartStop` id and the zone it arrives in. It writes the steps below, and a route may write them itself. A stop is a row named `Stop_<id>`: with that in `pickRow` the driver asks the chart which way to press next (`RouteChart.Toward`, the cursor's own links) instead of pressing down, so nothing counts presses; every step on the chart carries `"pauseUi":"pause"`, since the chart is a page of the pause menu; and the pause before the answer must outlast the .3 s the page waits before it takes one. ### 12.1 What reaches into another chapter's scene

A rebuild starts a scene from an empty one, and chapters stage into each other's scenes afterwards (a warp on the Tallow approach, a chart talk and a warp on Many Hands; chapters 1 and 2 also stage their own scenes after their builders). Every staged piece of every later chapter is gone after a rebuild, without a word, until someone stages it again. So each staging entry point carries `[Restage.Step(...)]` in its owner's file, and **one entry point runs them all, in the one order they need: `Restage.All`** (`S/Editor/World/Restage.cs`, marker `RESTAGE_OK`). **After rebuilding any scene, run `Restage.All` for it:**

```powershell
& .\scripts\exec-when-green.ps1 -Method Lattice.EditorTools.Restage.All -Marker RESTAGE_OK -Graphics -TimeoutSec 3000 -ExtraArgs @('-restage-scenes','ManyHands,ManyHandsHull')
```

Without `-restage-scenes` it runs every step on every scene. With it, only the steps that change those scenes run, still in order. A step that can be held to some of its scenes (`ScenesArg`) is given only those; one that can't (`ChapterOneStaging`, the Many Hands stagings) restages all of its scenes, and they join the run before anything runs (`RESTAGE_JOINED`), so every step for them runs, earlier ones included: restaging Harrowgate alone also restages Many Hands, where chapter 9 stages too, with chapters 6 to 8 (ChapterOneStaging leans Sorrel's rig and QaNov10Staging leans it further: the first alone would undo the second). The log states `RESTAGE_ORDER`, each scene's fingerprint before and after (`RESTAGE_BEFORE` and `RESTAGE_SCENE`: objects, a hash of the hierarchy, its navigation), every declared piece missing before (`RESTAGE_MISSING_BEFORE`) and put back (`RESTAGE_PUT_BACK`). It refuses, with no marker, when a step throws, a declared piece is still missing (`RESTAGE_STILL_MISSING`), or a step saved a scene its attribute doesn't name. `RestageTests` (PlayMode, one case per scene, found from the attributes) loads each built scene the player's way and fails by name on anything a step declares for it that it lacks: `.\scripts\focused.ps1 -SkipVerify -Label <yours> -Classes 'RestageTests'`.

**Registering a step.** One attribute on your staging entry point, in your own file, and nothing else to edit (a method run twice in the order carries two):

```csharp
// adds pieces to scenes built by someone else (the names are an example: list every scene it saves, and what it leaves in each)
[Restage.Step("chapter9.approach","ManyHands,ManyHandsHull,PicketOfTeeth,Harrowgate",After="chapter8.many-hands",Chapter=9,

public static void StageApproach()=>BatchTools.Run(()=>{Define();Approach.Stage();AssetDatabase.SaveAssets();Debug.Log("HOME_APPROACH_OK");});

// a whole scene made from another: rebuilt whenever its source is restaged
[Restage.Step("chapter9.decks-leaving","DecksLeaving",From="Hub_Decks",Chapter=9,ScenesArg="-home-zones",Leaves=new[]{"DecksLeaving: ... #1"})]
public static void BuildZones()=>BatchTools.Run(()=>{ ... });
```

## 13. Pitfalls

Things that fail without saying so:

1. **A missing yarn node** does nothing when talked to. A new `.yarn` file isn't compiled until the dialogue step runs.
2. *(No longer silent.)* A zone's music, objective line and build registration come from its `ZoneDef` and the open main quest: a later zone with no `musicId` warns `MUSIC_UNSET` and fails two tests; with no hand-written case it shows the story step or its own name, never the practice line; and the build list is read from the `ZoneDef` assets. What is left to forget is the zone's `ZoneLook` spec, and `WorldBuilder.Save` throws for that.
3. **A track named on a definition must exist by that exact title** in `docs/music/tracks.json`, with its source file: `MusicIntake.Stage` and `ContentScaffold.Check` throw `MUSIC_UNSOURCED` if not, and at run time a track named but never staged logs `MUSIC_MISSING`. The first thirteen zones play what `MusicDirector`'s own branches say whatever their definitions name.
4. **Component defaults are chapter 1's.** `WarpBeacon.requiredFlag="warpkey"`, `scene="Gullet_Tunnel"`; `Npc.postFlag="warpkey"`; `DiscoveryPoint.flag="hushwell.nursery"`, `report="HushwellNursery"`, `dialogueNode="SelaNursery"`; `LightCurtain.flag="wick.gateOpen"`; `YieldedCaptain` is all Hessa. Use the helpers, which set every field, or set every field.
5. **A `Flag` quest step set from code** (not `<<setFlag>>`) waits for the next load.
6. **One shop per zone**, found by the zone's id; at `ff99a7a`, with no asset by that name `<<shop>>` sells the starter stock at fallback prices, and prices follow parts sorted by slot, not the array's order (S2 is replacing both).
7. **`ArenaBuilder.Asset` overwrites what its lambda names**: a value tuned in the asset is undone by the next `Define()`, and several tests compare builder source with asset.
8. **Shader variants** not enabled on a material in the build are stripped. **Property blocks** aren't saved with a scene.
9. **Only static box and mesh colliders reach the navigation bake.**
10. **A scatter that moves instead of drops**, or a kit key inserted mid-list, shifts every later prop and breaks every route through the zone.
11. **`JsonUtility` ignores unknown route keys**, so the driver never complains: `tools/route_lint.py` does, and every replay's preflight runs it (section 10). A route's `sourceRevision` and step names are still free text nobody checks.
12. **`GenIntake` binds an enemy row only if the EnemyDef exists**; on "Unknown enemy" it has already saved the prefab and aborts the remaining rows.
13. *(No longer silent.)* A boss with no `Kit` throws `BOSS_UNREGISTERED` in the editor and logs it in a player; a boss with no sheet fails each shared boss test by name. Neither is given the Burrower's charge or Hessa's expectations any more. **A `Kit` is registered from a static field**, so don't call Unity APIs inside the `new Kit{...}` initializer itself (lambdas are fine: they run later).
14. *(Narrowed.)* The word `bridge` is banned only in chapters 1 and 2's nine script files (`ContentRegistry.FirstScripts`); the reason is written in `TheScriptStopsPromisingWhatIsntThere`. A new script may say it.
15. *(Replaced.)* The two "ten quests" pins now name chapters 1 and 2's ten by id and allow more. A new quest is walked by the quest tests as the first ten are; if its rewards overflow the carry cap those tests assume, that is a finding about the quest.
16. **`ZoneLook.Apply` after every zone rebuild**, or the zone ships with the builder's flat light. `TheChapterTwoZonesKeepTheirAuthoredLight` now catches it for every later zone.
17. **A `Membrane` is a blockout cube** with a generated material variant; chapter 2 replaced its renderers with strands. Don't leave the cube drawn.
18. **`tools/create_data_defs.py`, `WorldBuilder.BuildAll`, `GenIntake.Build`** are old whole-project entry points. `meshy.py` still prints "run PackStaging.StagePacks", which doesn't exist. `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` section 10 and `docs/art/PIPELINE.md` line 13 describe names and methods the code no longer has.

Structural limits a new chapter will run into: LRB's helpers are private and canyon-specific; `RoomDressing` knows chapter 1's seven zones only; the chapter's cards hold one main quest a chapter and two lines of closing words (section 8); the bespoke enemy behaviours in `EnemyBrain` (shield, cone, dive, death blast) and `RelayLantern`'s ward are still keyed on ids; `tools/portrait_pipeline.py` needs a Real-ESRGAN executable whose default path is in the FrostboundUnity repo; `DevLoadout`'s chapter loadouts wear tier 2 until S2's tiers are put in `ArrivalGear`, and know two heroes; there are no `docs/maps/` briefs for chapter 2's zones to copy; the driver's party logic assumes two members. Two that were on this list are gone: `scripts/look.ps1` and `ZoneLookProbe` take any built zone (`.\scripts\look.ps1 -Zones <Zone>,<Zone> [-Loadout chapter<N>] [-Folder Builds/logs/look/<name>]`, or no `-Zones` for every built zone: section 3.9, item 7), and a chapter's opening and closing cards come from its main quest (section 8).

What is no longer a shared switch (November 11, the framework pass): zone music, names, bright floors, build registration and looks; the objective line and the main-quest list; a boss's name, phases, moves, body and ending; an ordinary enemy's capsule, motion, drawn scale, death sound and name; and the test lists (section 11.2). The first two chapters' entries in those places are frozen: nothing is added to `MusicDirector.Authored`, `MusicIntake.SliceTracks`, `DisplayNames.zones/targets`, `SignStyle.BrightFloors`, `ZoneLook.Specs`, `BatchTools.FirstScenes`, `ObjectiveHud`'s `switch`, `QuestText.Briefs`, `StoryQuests.First`, `DevLoadout.FirstChapters` or the `First...` arrays of `ContentRegistry`. `Lattice.EditorTools.ContentScaffold.Apply` (marker `SCAFFOLD_OK changed=<n>`, idempotent) stamps the same facts onto chapters 1 and 2's own assets so data and code agree; nothing plays differently before or after it.

## 15. World mechanics

Parts a zone is built from that change the ground under the party. Chapter 3 made them for a river and a root island; none of them knows either. Each runs off a flag, so a save that has the flag loads with the place already changed and nothing played, and each has a test in `T/PlayMode/XylosMechanicsTests.cs` that builds it on a bare floor in `Arena_Ground`. Worked examples: `S/Editor/World/XylosBuilder.River.cs` and `XylosBuilder.Canopy.cs`.

### 15.1 Footing that slows (`Health.Hinder`)

`health.Hinder(pace,seconds=.15f)` claims a share of a body's walking pace for a moment; whatever lays it renews it each frame, the slowest live claim wins, and it lapses by itself. `Health.Pace` is read by a hero's `GroundMotor` and by `EnemyBrain.Move`. A dash, a dodge and a lunge keep their own speed, so there is always a way out. Use it for anything a body stands in: water, a spore cloud, a puddle, tar. (`Health.SlowUntil` is still the 0.4 that a skill or a ward lays on an enemy.)

### 15.2 Water (`WaterVolume`, `WaterKit`)

A zone's ground is one height function, so water is a level over it. `S/World/WaterVolume.cs` is the body of water; `S/Editor/World/WaterKit.cs` builds it:

```csharp
var look=WaterKit.Look("xylos-river-water",shallow,deep,foam,deepAt:1,ripple:1.7f,sparkle:.14f); // the public look (D131), in Resources/WorldMaterials
var sheet=WaterKit.Sheet(kit,"Middle channel",20,200,74,122,1,0,look,p=>InThisChannel(p)); // drawn where the ground is under the level; highest: for water that rises
var water=WaterKit.Volume(sheet,0,(centre,size,yaw),...);                               // loose rectangles the water lies within
water.drainFlag="xylos.sluiceA";water.drainedLevel=-.7f;water.drainSeconds=3;           // optional: goes down on a flag, for good
WaterKit.Deep(kit,water,20,200,74,122,p=>InThisChannel(p));                             // walls and holes in the paths where it is over .55 m
```

- **Wading.** A body on foot whose feet are 0.12 m under the surface inside a reach wades at `wadePace` (0.62): heroes and enemies. Ships and anything with `EnemyDef.motion==Hover` are over it. It splashes on the way in (`water_splash`) and leaves rings. Where the water is comes from the ground itself, so reaches are rough boxes; keep one water's reaches off another water's bed.
- **The look.** The sheet's second uv set carries each point's ground height and depth; the toon shader's water reads the depth (clear shallows going to an opaque deep colour over `deepAt` metres, foam where the ground comes up through it), and the volume writes it again whenever the level moves. So a drained channel's shore is where its bed says. A `BiomePatch` on the sheet lets licensed water take it over locally by name. `ZoneKit.Water` makes a simpler sheet for a pond that never moves.
- **Deep water.** `WaterKit.Deep` stands boxes in the water wherever it is deeper than `from`: under one holder that is a `Membrane` (deep water is a seal like any other, so the world tests that open every seal open it too), **not static** (the bake walks the bed under them, so nothing is baked again when the water goes), each a carving `NavMeshObstacle`, and only waist-high, so shots cross a channel. Gone when the volume drains; there for good if it has no `drainFlag`.
- **Baking.** Wrap the bake in `WaterKit.WithOpenWater(()=>kit.BakeNavigation(...))` so the must-reach points are checked with every channel down.
- **Anything else that moves water** calls `water.MoveTo(height,seconds)`. `WaterVolume.DepthAt(point)` answers "how deep here" for all waters.
- Logs: `WATER_DRAINING <flag>`, `WATER_DRAINED <flag>`. For stills of a drained river, `XylosBuilder.BuildWild -xylos-drained` builds it that way (never commit that scene).

Things the river found out:

- **Put the water north of the ground that is walked.** The fixed camera sees about twelve metres north of the party and five south, and nothing further however tall. The first layout had a 30 m bank with the channel beyond it and a jungle beyond that: every frame was floor. The river as built is strips of land 16 to 20 m wide, each with a channel along its north shore and its path within a few strides of that shore; the far jungle is only ever in an overview. Trees that have to be in a play frame stand on a strip's north edge, north of its walked line: a trunk there is the back of the frame, and its crown is over water.
- **Keep the glint's grain small.** `Look`'s `ripple` is the noise's frequency: at 0.5 the sparkle is two-metre slabs of white lying on the water; at 1.5 and over it is glints. Keep `sparkle` under 0.15 outdoors and under 0.1 under a roof.
- **A reach never lies over another water's bed.** A drained channel beside a full one would be waded as if it were full. Where two waters meet along a slanted line (a gate, a weir), turn the reach to lie along it (`(centre,size,yaw)`).
- **A sill that holds two levels apart has to stand above the lower one**, or the higher sheet ends in mid-air when its neighbour drains. If nobody should cross it, make it a dam with a `BlockAlong` (the river's weirs); if it is a ford, it is simply ground under the wade depth.
- **`Gap(a,b,level)` finds a gate's two ends from the ground itself**, so a sluice or a weir never has to be told how wide the water is.
- **`Rafts(kit,name,surface,level,...)`** (in `XylosBuilder.River.cs`) scatters floating leaf on a water as its children: pads on a channel, duckweed that rides a flood up a hall. A wide sheet of calm water is the plainest thing in a frame without them.
- **The damp layer's texture is dark** (`ground-damp`, mean 0.45): tinted under about 0.7 it lies on a floor as black holes, and a flat thing a hand above a waterline (a gravel bar) is painted with it from edge to edge. Tint it up, and keep it off anything meant to read as something else.
- **What stands on a wall obeys the wall's rule.** The Canopy's ground stays under the sightline to the floor behind it; root columns stood on that ground did not, and hid the party on a ramp. `Sightline(p)` in `XylosBuilder.Canopy.cs` is the height anything at a plan point may reach; a column is cut to it or left out, and nothing is laid on the camera's side of a floor. **And the rule has to sit under the camera's real line, with room to spare.** The camera stands 12.1 m over the hero and 15 m back, so its line to a hero's feet rises .81 a metre. The Canopy's ground climbed at .82 from six tenths over the floor, and its columns were allowed a metre over the line: both passed their own rule, and in the player the basin's near wall and a row of columns hid the party to the chest and filled half the frame, two metres under the lens (the Floodcrown fell with nobody on screen). What is *drawn* on the camera's side of a floor is now its own function (`CanopySeen`, handed to `kit.Terrain` as `drawn`; `SeenOver` is the rule): a kerb at the floor's edge, then a slope that stays under the line of sight and never comes within five metres of the lens. Bodies and the bake still meet the steeper ground, which nobody sees. Two things go with a lower drawn ground. `LensCap(p)` is the lens itself: the camera hangs 15 m back from a hero on any floor, so a wall that is on nobody's line to a floor can still stand in front of the glass (the basin's west wall did). And `kit.Terrain(..., drawn, floorWithin)`: the terrain builder calls any easy slope below `cliffTop` floor, textures it as floor and lets the ground kit grow grass on it, so a zone that draws gentle banks hands it a function that is positive on its real floors (`p=>-CanopyFloor(p,out _)`), and everything outside is drawn as rock (`NothingFloatsOrSinksIn` finds the grass otherwise: "hangs over nothing at all"). A zone carved from a solid the same way owes the same check: put a hero at the camera-side edge of its lowest floor and look.

A later chapter: a swamp ruin is a `WaterVolume` whose `drainFlag` a pump sets; a lava channel is the same volume with another `Look` and a hazard laid over its reaches; a frozen one never wades because nothing is under its surface.

### 15.3 A switch a hero works (`FlowSwitch`, `ShiftAnchor`; base `WorldSwitch`)

Like a relay lantern, the switch is the answer and not the hand. The hand is a `DiscoveryPoint` beside it (`kit.Discovery`): it carries the prompt, the talk, the quest's count and the save, the objective panel finds it by its `report`, and it sets the flag, itself or through its conversation's last line (`<<setFlag>>`: use that when the step is a `Flag` step). The switch watches the flag and plays when it goes from unset to set.

- `FlowSwitch{flag,wheel,axis,turns,seconds,sound,shows,point}`: a wheel that turns and a rush of water (`water_rush`); log `SLUICE_OPEN <flag>` (or `marker`). **`shows`** is the stretch of water it changes, as a world box: while a hero stands at its `point`, and again as the wheel turns, the view draws back to take it in, which is how a player sees what a wheel will do before turning it. Stand the switch at the head of its own water as well. `WaterKit.Wheel` makes the wheel. A swamp's pump or a forge's coolant valve is this with another prompt and sound.
- `ShiftAnchor{flag,whole,severed,tremorSeconds,tremorAmplitude,answers,sound,point}`: the place lurches (a camera impulse and a tremor, `root_groan`, dust at each of `answers`), `whole` is swapped for `severed`; log `ANCHOR_CUT <flag>`; event `ShiftAnchor.Lurched`. **Doors are not its business**: each door is a solid thing with a carving `NavMeshObstacle`, switched by a `FlagGate` on the same flag (see `RootDoor` and the door table in `XylosBuilder.Canopy.cs`; a door that is shut only between two flags hangs under a holder the second flag removes). Bake with every door switched off.

## ZoneKit

`S/Editor/World/ZoneKit*.cs` (November 11). LRB's private helpers, lifted into one shared, region-neutral class so chapters 3 to 10 don't each carry a copy (section 3.1's warning). LRB itself is unchanged and keeps its own. `XylosBuilder` (chapter 3) is the worked example: `XylosBuilder.Rootmarket.cs` for a town and `XylosBuilder.Nearspace.cs` for a flight zone; `XylosBuilder.River.cs` and `XylosBuilder.Canopy.cs` are the second world engineer's wild zone and dungeon on the same kit, with `WaterKit` for water that moves.

**A kit is one zone's context.** LRB kept `height`, `CliffTop` and `fadeTall` in statics that the zone built before could leave set; a kit carries them, and everything that needs them is called through it. What needs no zone is static.

```csharp
var kit=ZoneKit.Ground("Driftreed",ReedHeight,3.2f);          // name, the ground under a plan point, where level ground stops being floor; fadeTall on
var scene=WorldBuilder.Begin(kit.zone);
var land=kit.Terrain("Reed rafts",-60,60,-20,110,.5f,floorMaterial,wallMaterial,ReedTone);   // GroundTerrain's meshes, stood in the scene; any floor and wall material
kit.Water("Mere",-60,60,-20,110,1,-.7f,waterMaterial);         // a still sheet where the ground is below a level (WaterKit is for water that moves)
WorldBuilder.Spawn("Arrival",kit.On(0,4));                     // kit.On(x,z,up) lifts a plan point onto the ground
kit.Prop(ZoneKit.Kit("ReedHall","CanyonHouse"),new Vector3(0,0,60),9,180);                   // the key if its prefab is in, else the stand-in
kit.Person("Weaver",new Vector3(4,0,52),200,"wendmire.airFound");                            // WeaverFirst / WeaverRepeat / WeaverPost
kit.Gate("Reed gate — The Drowned Waystation",new Vector3(40,0,30),"DrownedWaystation","Arrival","wendmire.matsPromised","Ask the weavers first");
kit.BakeNavigation(new Bounds(new Vector3(0,3,45),new Vector3(130,24,140)),new Vector2(0,4),new[]{new Vector2(4,50),new Vector2(38,30)});
var ground=GroundKit.Open(kit.zone,land.FloorFilter,17,3.2f);  // last, once everything stands: layers, flat details, cover, moving shade (GroundKit.cs)
WorldBuilder.Save(scene,kit.zone);                             // refuses a zone with no ZoneLook spec: [ZoneLook.Source] static void Looks(){ZoneLook.Register(...);}
```

A flight zone is `ZoneKit.Flight("SporeVeil")`: no ground, `fadeTall` off, everything at `ZoneKit.FlightPlane`.

Things the first region on the kit found out:

- **A platform's edge can be the wall.** Rootmarket's ground is ovals of floor in water; each has a knee-high rim of root along every edge, steep on both faces (`RootHeight`). The hero's step is .35 m, so a .5 m rim stops a walk with something drawn and no `Boundary` box anywhere. Leave the rim out only where a built deck crosses (`Openings`), and give the deck's own sides a drawn rail on a `BlockAlong`.
- **Check what you place against the ground you wrote.** With a floor made of ovals, a coordinate a metre out is a townsperson standing on a rim, or a platform that touches nothing. `OnTheFloor(...)` in `XylosBuilder.Rootmarket.cs` throws by name before anything is built.
- **Put the zone's places in named constants** (`RuvaAtMooring`, `RopeAt`, `Barges`, `Beside(s,starboard)`): the scatter's keep-off, the navigation's must-reach list, LookDev's views, the map brief and the route generator all want the same numbers.
- **A flight lane that bends is laid out by distance along it** (`XylosBuilder.Lane`, `Beside`), and walled with `FlightCorridor`. One axis-aligned box round a diagonal lane leaves two corners of empty sky a player can fly into.
- **A counted story step needs something for the objective's arrow to find.** The panel points at the nearest `DiscoveryPoint` still wanted under a step's `report`. Where the world reports the step itself (a barge cut free reports `SeedBarge`), stand a point with that report and the same flag beside it, its `previewEncounter` set so it is never offered (`XylosBuilder.SeedBarge`).
- **People in more than one place** over a chapter are one `Npc` per place and a `S/World/Whereabouts.cs` on each (`from`, `awayFrom`/`backAt`, `goneAt`; decided at load unless `live`).

## Ground kit

A fixed three-quarter camera shows mostly floor, and a floor of one tone with a few props reads as an empty plane. `python tools/look_density.py Builds/quality/look/<run> --max-blob 0.22` measures it on LookDev stills: the largest connected stretch of plain floor as a share of the frame (the dressed Decks: .05 to .21). Every play-zoom still of a new ground zone should come in at or under .22, **with and without** `-local-biomes`, and then be looked at: the number is a floor, not the goal.

A new zone's recipe, at the end of its builder (after everything that stands is placed) or as a step in `GroundStaging`:

```csharp
var kit=GroundKit.Open("Mirefen",GroundKit.FindFloor(),seed:7);                    // the open scene's "... floor" mesh
kit.ClearLane(Boardwalk,1.6f).Look("Canyon",rockTexture);                           // nothing stands in a lane; the local grass palette and the rocks' texture
kit.Layers(null,1.1f,new GroundKit.Layer("pavers",new Color(.9f,.86f,.7f),5),     // up to three layers over the floor's own texture
    new GroundKit.Layer("litter",new Color(.7f,.8f,.5f,.85f),3.2f),new GroundKit.Layer("damp",new Color(.5f,.56f,.5f),6));
kit.PaintPath(0,Boardwalk,2.2f,wear:1,broken:.4f);                                  // an old way, whole in places
foreach(var hut in GroundKit.Named("MireHut"))kit.PaintAround(0,hut,2.4f);          // a threshold at every door
kit.PaintPatches(1,metres:6,cover:.35f,where:p=>GroundKit.Smooth(3,5,kit.Lane(p)));// noise-shaped patches off the way
kit.PaintFoot(2,reach:2.5f).ShadeRelief(MireRelief);                                // wet at the banks' feet; the height function's relief, painted
kit.Decals(new[]{"CracksA","CracksB"},4,p=>GroundKit.Smooth(1,2.5f,kit.Edge(p)),2,4,new Color(.8f,.8f,.75f,.9f));
kit.Decals(new[]{"Stain"},2,null,3,5,new Color(.3f,.4f,.35f,.7f),strong:false);  // quiet marks only keep off signs and props
kit.Strip("Tracks",new[]{Den,Ford},0,3.4f,new Color(.4f,.42f,.4f,.8f));kit.Puddle(new Vector2(4,12),.9f,.6f);
kit.Cover(new[]{"Reeds","Fern"},30,p=>1-GroundKit.Smooth(1,3,kit.Edge(p)),.3f,.55f,new Color(.7f,.9f,.6f),clump:3);
kit.Rocks(6,null,.08f,.3f,new Color(.8f,.8f,.75f));kit.LampPools(2.5f,new Color(1,.8f,.55f,.4f));
kit.MovingShade("dapple",size:11,drift:Vector2.zero,strength:.6f,threshold:.35f,edgeWidth:.04f,sway:new Vector2(.4f,.2f));
kit.Finish();                                                                       // throws GROUND_PROBLEM and leaves the scene unsaved on a fault
```

What the kit keeps to, so a recipe doesn't have to:

- **Signs, prompts, people, props.** Nothing lies under a floor sign or stands on the ground the camera sees it across; strong details and all cover keep off prompts and spawns; cover keeps out of declared lanes, off whatever stands (by drawn box), out of the line between the camera and anyone, and, at .32 m or taller, out of the middle of a fight (D131). `Finish` checks all of it again and refuses on a fault, and on any cover over .6 m, anything solid under the root, or a weight that isn't a number.
- **Names.** Every renderer it makes is called `Floor detail ...`, the name the suite's box tests already skip for flat things: a batch's box says only that the ground under it slopes. Tufts are `BiomePatch`es (public card fallback; `LocalBiomeBake.BuildPatches -biome-zones a,b` bakes the licensed grass for them, palette from `kit.Look`).
- **Order.** It reads the scene as it stands, so it runs **last**: after the zone's builder, `ZoneLook.Apply`, and for chapter 1 after `RoomDressing`, `ChapterOneStaging` and `QaNov10Staging`. A rebuild of the landform makes a new floor mesh and puts the plain material back: rerun `GroundStaging.Apply`, then `LocalBiomeBake.BuildPatches` if the licensed packs are installed.
- **Draw order.** Flat details blend at `Transparent-20`, before danger markings (`Transparent-10`), so a marking always lies over them.

Traps this found:

- **Vertex colour is not converted.** A tint written as it should look comes out pale and chalky in a vertex: the kit bakes its tints linear (`Baked`). `TownTone`-style floor tones were tuned by eye against this and are left alone.
- **Don't compare a texture importer's size.** The project's own texture rules set it, so a "fix it if it differs" check reimported two textures seven hundred times in one run. The kit compares only wrap, mips, alpha and colour space, once a run.
- **A zone's floor material is written whole each run** (`new Material(source)`, tuned, `CopySerialized` over the kept asset). `CopyPropertiesFromMaterial` onto the existing copy left Lantern Gorge's floor without its layers on a second run.
- **A layer's tint is a material colour (sRGB), a detail's tint is baked to match.** Pick both as the colour you want to see before lighting; the canyon's floor is about (.84,.62,.47) on the road and (.66,.46,.37) off it.
- **A worn patch is set by measure, not by eye on the swatch.** Multiply the layer's mean (linear) by its tint (linear) and compare with the floor's albedo: at three times the floor's brightness with an ink line (Hushwell's first sand) a patch reads as paper laid on the floor; at the floor's own hue and 1.4 times, a dark cave goes one murky tone. In a dim zone, tell layers apart by hue as well as value: Hushwell ended with red-brown dust, warm rubble at about twice and grey bedrock at about 2.5 times, no ink, a `Fringe` of grit, and `Erase` to keep the top layer (scree) off a way it would bury. Pick a layer with structure of its own (cracks, stones): a near-flat texture tinted pale is a cut-out.
- **Three layers is the limit** (one sampler set, one uv set). A fourth material is a decal kind or a second zone-specific texture swap, not a fourth weight.
- **A cave, or any floor drawn in pieces.** `GroundKit.Open(zone,mesh,seed,maxFloor,wholeLandform:true,isFloor:p=>...)` takes a rule for what is floor on a landform whose rock tops are level too (Hushwell: `HushwellLayout.Clear(p)<0` at the level's height). Where the floor is drawn by pieces cut from the painted mesh (Hushwell's lit chunks), `kit.DrawnAs(pieces,plainMaterial)` gives each piece its weights and the layered material. `kit.Glows(per100,where,small,large,light)` lays small additive lights (glow-fungus, crystal dust): lit details barely show in the dark, so underground the variety has to come from layers and from light. `RoomDressing.Apply` rebuilds the chunks, so `GroundStaging.Apply -ground-zones Hushwell` follows it.
